## Per driver

### Soy-maize safrinha window
**Mechanism.** Soy is the first crop and maize follows it on the same field. The farmer must coordinate soy sowing, maturity, harvest capacity, maize planting and topdressing within a wet-season window that closes toward the dry season. Embrapa's Santa Carmem-MT field experiment identifies about 20 February as a practical upper bound for high-potential second-crop maize in the Cerrado-Amazon transition, not a universal national date. (read) [Source: Lange et al., 2024, https://rbms.sede.embrapa.br/ojs/article/download/1345/1615/11443]
**Numbers.** In the experiment conducted in 2018, maize sown 19 January yielded 2.33 Mg/ha more than maize sown 27 February; the study used five replications and a specific farm in Santa Carmem, MT. (read)
**Who carries the cost.** Owner and tenant lose yield and input efficiency; tenant may have rent due from the soy crop before maize returns cash; lender and co-op carry repayment and barter exposure. (read)
**Counterexample.** Weather and harvest delay are not always controllable, and the Aprosoja-MT 2024 alternative-crop guide was produced for years when late maize becomes uncertain. Sorghum, millet, sesame, sunflower, brachiaria or crotalaria can replace grain maize, but each has different market, cover and nematode trade-offs. (read) [Source: https://siteapi.aprosoja.com.br/storage/arquivos/4/240724142657.pdf]

### Soy maturity group
**Mechanism.** Maturity group is a calendar control: the selected cultivar changes harvest date and therefore the available second-crop window. It should be optimized for the whole sequence, not soy yield alone. (read) [Source: https://www.scielo.br/j/resr/a/Cp5dGMsPtgBh4G3Mz48SzFs?lang=en]
**Numbers.** In the Dois Vizinhos-PR trials in 2016/17 and 2017/18, MG 5.1-5.3 sown 1 October provided financial balance; MG 6.0 sown mid-September was the most lucrative tested arrangement and allowed maize sowing in mid-February; 15 October soybean sowing made maize uneconomic as a second summer crop. (read)
**Who carries the cost.** Owner and agronomist make cultivar and sow-date choices; seed supplier sells the package; lender is exposed if planting falls outside ZARC. (read)
**Counterexample.** “Choose the earliest group” is not a general rule: the PR study favored MG 6.0 at an earlier sowing date, while later soybean sowing defeated the second crop irrespective of maturity selection. The result is from one PR location and two seasons, not a whole-Cerrado cultivar table. (read)

### Cotton as second crop and pesticide load
**Mechanism.** Cotton competes with maize for the post-soy slot, normally demanding earlier sowing and more specialized machinery, working capital and pest management. In MT, a 2020-edition IMAmt/AMPA manual said about 85% of cotton was second crop and most area was planted in January; that is historical practice evidence, not a current state share. (read) [Source: https://imamt.org.br/wp-content/uploads/2020/06/manualdeboaspraticas2020-4ed-vf-web.pdf]
**Numbers.** Kynetec FarmTrak counted 107 treatments per cotton property in 2025/26 versus 95 in 2024/25; average cost per treatment was R$4,209 versus R$3,472, and the 2025/26 cotton pesticide market was R$8.217bn. These are survey/market figures, not a per-hectare national measure. (read) [Source: https://portaldoagronegocio.com.br/agricultura/algodao/noticias/mercado-de-defensivos-para-algodao-bate-recorde-de-r-8-2-bilhoes-na-safra-2025-26]
**Who carries the cost.** Owner bears machinery and crop risk, tenant bears leased-land cashflow, lender carries larger operating-credit exposure, agronomist manages the spray calendar, and gin/buyer sets quality and delivery conditions. (read)
**Counterexample.** Cotton is not a universal rotation fix: it can help break soybean cyst nematode but can itself be damaged by *P. brachyurus*. In addition, MT cotton area fell 11.18% in 2025/26 despite a record yield, showing economics can outweigh biological fit. (read) [Source: https://revistacultivar.com.br/artigos/manejo-de-nematoides-em-algodao]

### Corn leafhopper and stunt
**Mechanism.** *Dalbulus maidis* transmits pale and red stunt pathogens. Volunteer maize bridges crop cycles, and asynchronous planting lets the vector move between older and younger crops. Embrapa recommends coordinated planting, tolerant or resistant hybrids, volunteer-maize control and early monitoring, because insecticide-only control is insufficient. (read) [Source: https://www.embrapa.br/busca-de-noticias/-/noticia/115108046/cigarrinha-do-milho-causa-prejuizo-de-258-bilhoes-de-dolares-ao-pais-em-quatro-anos]
**Numbers.** For 2020-2024, the Embrapa/Epagri/CNA analysis estimated an average 22.7% annual national corn loss, or 31.8 Mt and US$6.5bn per year; 2020/21 loss was 28.9%, and 2023/24 was 16.7%. Insecticide cost rose 19% during that analysis period to above US$9/ha. (read)
**Who carries the cost.** All neighboring growers share the landscape risk; individual farmer pays for monitoring, seed and sprays; co-ops and agronomists are needed to coordinate neighboring schedules. (read)
**Counterexample.** Loss pressure varies by year: the Embrapa analysis estimated national loss falling from 28.9% in 2020/21 to 16.7% in 2023/24. Those are retrospective national estimates, not current local incidence or a 2026/27 field forecast. (read)

### Soybean cyst nematode
**Mechanism.** Maize used to be a summer rotation break for soy cyst nematode; as maize moved into the autumn succession slot after soy, that break was lost. Cultivar resistance is race-specific, so repeating the same source of resistance can select races not controlled by it. (read) [Source: https://www.embrapa.br/busca-de-noticias/-/noticia/63558908/controle-de-nematoides-fitoparasitos-rotacao-de-culturas-e-uma-das-indicacoes]
**Numbers.** Embrapa's 2021 synthesis said one year of rotation with maize, sugarcane, brachiaria or cotton reduced viable cysts about 70%; 25% of soybean cultivars carried resistance, mostly to races 1 and 3; 1-3 cysts per 100 cm3 soil could already cause damage. (read)
**Who carries the cost.** Landowner or long-lease tenant absorbs multi-year rotation costs; agronomist and seed supplier must identify race and resistance match; diagnostic lab tests soil/root samples. (read)
**Counterexample.** A visible yellow patch is not diagnosis: Fundação Rio Verde advised in October 2026 that drought, compaction and nutrition can mimic cyst damage and recommended nematode analysis. Presence in a sample is not equivalent to a measured percentage yield loss. (read) [Source: https://fundacaorioverde.com.br/nematoide-do-cisto-da-soja-um-alerta-para-a-safra-2026-27]

### Root-knot and lesion nematodes
**Mechanism.** *P. brachyurus* can multiply on maize and soybean, making the soy-maize succession a host sequence; crotalaria or millet may break the cycle but sacrifice cash-crop revenue or require a different planting slot. Embrapa notes response varies by species and even brachiaria type. (read) [Source: https://www.embrapa.br/busca-de-noticias/-/noticia/63558908/controle-de-nematoides-fitoparasitos-rotacao-de-culturas-e-uma-das-indicacoes]
**Numbers.** An AgroMax survey of 2,738 soil/root samples from 44 MT municipalities in 2023/24 found *P. brachyurus* in 99.2% of samples; this is sample incidence, not prevalence among all farms or a yield-loss percentage. (read, survey reported by supplier Bela Cereais: https://belacereais.com.br/2026/soja/nematoides-ja-estao-em-99-das-areas-de-soja-em-mato-grosso-aponta-estudo/)
**Who carries the cost.** Owner and tenant bear the foregone maize income and sampling costs; input sellers may benefit from nematicide/seed treatment sales, so advice should be checked against field test results. (read)
**Counterexample.** A cover crop's nematode response does not guarantee positive margin: the 2024 Aprosoja-MT guide lists alternative crops with variable uses and constraints, including seed/harvest handling and biomass differences. (read) [Source: https://siteapi.aprosoja.com.br/storage/arquivos/4/240724142657.pdf]

### Cercospora leaf blight
**Mechanism.** Cercospora leaf blight, target spot and rust are separate disease problems; a recommendation that simply counts “fungicide disease pressure” will not distinguish pathogen, disease timing, seed quality or spray coverage. (read) [Source: https://fundacaoms.org.br/pesquisa-inedita-apresenta-impacto-de-doencas-na-produtividade-da-soja]
**Numbers.** Fundação MS presented trial losses of 10.4 sacks/ha for Cercospora, 15 sacks/ha for target spot and 6.6 sacks/ha for Asian rust across four MS locations on 2024-05-23; raising application volume from 50 to 120-150 L/ha recovered 10.3-10.9 sacks/ha in the tested conditions. (read)
**Who carries the cost.** Farmer pays yield and quality losses; agronomist owns diagnosis and application setup; seed producer bears seed-quality risk. (read)
**Counterexample.** Target spot had the larger measured loss than Cercospora in the same MS report, so a Cercospora-first rotation prescription is not supported by that trial. It is not a national disease-loss survey. (read)

### Asian rust and fungicide resistance
**Mechanism.** The humid South supports survival of volunteer soy during winter, while the drier Cerrado has less volunteer-soy survival and more opportunity for crop escape. In PR, early sowing can increase exposure time; multisite mixtures help manage fungicide resistance. (read) [Source: https://www.embrapa.br/busca-de-noticias/-/noticia/106270547/clima-e-falhas-em-manejo-explicam-aumento-da-incidencia-de-ferrugem-da-soja-no-parana]
**Numbers.** Embrapa reported 144 2025/26-season records as of 2026-01-09, including 88 PR, 44 MS and 5 RS; PR had 41 at the same point in the previous season. The report explicitly says rising records do not prove loss of control. Separately, a 2025 review found SdhC-I86F in the majority of sampled *P. pachyrhizi* populations in 2020/21 and 2022/23 across eight states, associated with reduced fluxapyroxad sensitivity; this is a resistance signal, not a current statewide failure rate. (read) [Source: https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40858-025-00719-y]
**Who carries the cost.** Farmer pays spray and field-monitoring cost; co-op agronomists aggregate local alerts; neighboring farms affect inoculum survival. (read)
**Counterexample.** A rust-ranking tool should not transfer South pressure to the Cerrado: Embrapa says humid winter sustains volunteer soy in South and rust reports are more numerous there, while Cerrado rust can be less economically important than target spot. (read)

### Rural credit and Plano Safra
**Mechanism.** Brazilian crop years run July to June, and controlled-credit availability and rate depend on the annual Plano Safra and its implementing CMN rules. Credit affects working capital for soy inputs and the following crop; if a controlled line is unavailable, the farmer may rely on a co-op, trader or input supplier. (read) [Source: https://www.gov.br/agricultura/pt-br/assuntos/politica-agricola/plano-safra/2026-2027/filipeta-plano-safra-2026-2027-defeso.pdf]
**Numbers.** MAPA's 2026/27 plan listed R$525.1bn enterprise credit overall, of which R$384.9bn was custeio/commercialization and R$140.2bn investment. Published annual rates included 12.5% for enterprise custeio, 9% for Pronamp and 8.5% for RenovAgro pasture recovery/conversion, all for the 2026/27 plan. (read)
**Who carries the cost.** Treasury funds interest equalization; farmer pays the contracted rate and any gap after limited controlled resources; lender screens collateral and compliance; co-op/trader may capture margin through replacement finance. (read)
**Counterexample.** The headline credit envelope is not the same as money available to every producer: line, borrower eligibility, bank appraisal and disbursement timing matter. A larger aggregate plan does not prove an individual farmer can finance a cover crop or delayed-payoff rotation. (read)

### PROAGRO loan protection
**Mechanism.** Proagro protects eligible agricultural operating-credit obligations after covered losses. It does not necessarily replace all expected farm revenue. ZARC, enrolled credit amount and historical claims shape eligibility and payment. (read) [Source: https://www.bcb.gov.br/estabilidadefinanceira/exibenormativo?numero=5224&tipo=Resolu%C3%A7%C3%A3o%20CMN]
**Numbers.** Resolution 5.224 set the beneficiary enrollment cap at R$200,000 per crop year effective 2025-07-01. It also established that claims history involving at least 60% of enrolled sums and at least R$250,000 indemnified in the past five crop years can trigger exclusion conditions. (read)
**Who carries the cost.** Farmer pays the additional premium and uncovered loss; lender acts as program agent; government carries the public guarantee exposure. (read)
**Counterexample.** A grower with sufficient own capital may opt out and self-insure, while a financed farmer may still have uncovered production costs or revenue risk beyond the eligible amount. Proagro's cap and conditions mean it is not a substitute for whole-farm crop insurance. (read)

### Rural insurance subsidy (PSR)
**Mechanism.** PSR subsidizes part of the insurance premium, not the loss itself; an annual budget block can make the subsidy unavailable even where the farmer wants to buy cover. (read) [Source: https://globorural.globo.com/credito-e-investimento/noticia/2026/06/governo-bloqueia-metade-do-orcamento-do-seguro-rural-em-2026.ghtml]
**Numbers.** On 2026-06-09, the government blocked R$461.6m of the R$991.1m revised budget, equivalent to 46.5%; 2025 execution was R$565.4m, supporting 3.2m hectares and a little more than 60,000 policies. (read)
**Who carries the cost.** Farmer pays the unsubsidized premium, deductible and uncovered loss; Treasury pays subsidy subject to available budget; insurer prices the risk. (read)
**Counterexample.** The 2025 program still covered 3.2m hectares despite budget volatility, and the 2026 block was reported as not a permanent cancellation. Subsidy exposure is therefore a timing/availability risk rather than proof that no farmer can obtain private insurance. (read)

### ZARC compliance
**Mechanism.** ZARC maps sowing risk by municipality, soil type and cultivar cycle. From 2025-07-01 it also conditions agricultural operating credit, so agronomic timing and loan eligibility are linked. (read) [Source: https://normativos.bcb.gov.br/Votos/CMN/202535/Voto_do_CMN_35_2025.pdf]
**Numbers.** The CMN decision effective 2025-07-01 extended ZARC observance beyond the previously required small Proagro-linked operations to agricultural custeio credit more broadly. A single state-level sowing date cannot represent all soil/cultivar/municipality windows. (read)
**Who carries the cost.** Farmer loses credit/insurance eligibility or bears greater yield risk if planting outside the applicable window; lender must check compliance; agronomist must match cultivar and municipality. (read)
**Counterexample.** A fully self-financed grower may choose outside a ZARC credit condition, accepting agronomic risk; and ZARC is not a uniform national calendar. (read)

### Legal reserve and land-use opportunity cost
**Mechanism.** The Forest Code sets a native-vegetation minimum on rural property; the land obligation follows successors and affects the tenant's area available under lease. This is a property-level biome rule, not a uniform Brazil-wide farm percentage. (read) [Source: https://www.planalto.gov.br/ccivil_03/_ato2011-2014/2012/lei/l12651.htm]
**Numbers.** Under Article 12, minimum legal reserve is 80% on forest properties, 35% on Cerrado properties and 20% on campos gerais inside the Legal Amazon; it is 20% in other regions. For illustration only, a 1,000-ha Cerrado property in the Legal Amazon starts with a 350-ha reserve minimum before considering other legal provisions and APP. (read; arithmetic derived from statute)
**Who carries the cost.** Owner carries opportunity cost and compliance; tenant receives less cultivable area; lender and buyer may require environmental status documentation. (read)
**Counterexample.** Legal reserve is not always an on-farm area cap: the law provides compensation routes for certain deficits, and exceptions can apply based on property history and size. The property's biome and CAR cannot be replaced by a state-level average. (read)

### CAR registration and credit gate
**Mechanism.** CAR is a required environmental registry and its boundary becomes a reference layer for credit screening and traceability. Registration is not equivalent to validation, land title, or verified plot-level commodity segregation. (read) [Source: https://www.planalto.gov.br/ccivil_03/_ato2011-2014/2012/lei/l12651.htm]
**Numbers.** Secondary reporting of end-2025 SFB data counted 8,154,567 CARs covering 718.9 Mha, with 480,700 completed analyses, or 5.9%; those totals are registry coverage, not the number of validated farms or hectares. (read, https://portaldbo.com.br/select_post/conclusoes-do-cadastro-ambiental-rural-car-enfim-decolam)
**Who carries the cost.** Owner supplies registry data and resolves mismatches; lender and co-op/trader screen; buyer may require additional geolocation; public agencies bear analysis backlogs. (read)
**Counterexample.** A pending CAR may still satisfy a rule that checks only registration status, while a registered polygon can still be inaccurate or unresolved. Do not treat a CAR count as proof of traceable compliant soy. (read)

### EUDR traceability
**Mechanism.** EUDR binds the operator placing the covered product on the EU market, which then passes geolocation and legality requests through exporter, trader, co-op and farm contracts. CAR may help identify the property but does not itself fulfil the due-diligence statement or prove that a specific lot came from a compliant field. (read) [Source: https://environment.ec.europa.eu/topics/forests/deforestation/eu-deforestation-regulation-eudr_en]
**Numbers.** The EU Commission's page lists soy among covered commodities, the 2020-12-31 deforestation cutoff, plot geolocation and digital due diligence; application dates shown are 2026-12-30 for medium/large operators and 2027-06-30 for micro/small operators. (read)
**Who carries the cost.** The EU operator has the direct legal duty; exporters/traders usually build traceability systems and may pass costs or rejection risk to suppliers; farmer provides field records and polygons. (read)
**Counterexample.** EUDR applies to the EU-bound chain, not every domestic or China-bound tonne. Separately, ABIOVE announced in January 2026 that it would withdraw from the Soy Moratorium, but that source was URL-verified and not read here; the private enforcement picture is therefore unsettled and should not be represented as a settled legal change. (URL verified, not read) [Source: https://valorinternational.globo.com/agribusiness/news/2026/01/05/abiove-says-it-will-withdraw-from-the-soy-moratorium.ghtml]

### Lease rent and tenant break-even
**Mechanism.** Soy-sack rent creates a fixed physical claim on crop output, due regardless of tenant yield. A short lease can make an investment whose payback extends beyond the contract unattractive; this is a plausible constraint, but direct causal evidence linking tenure to rotation choice was not found. (read)
**Numbers.** Aprosoja-MS's 2026/27 model used 15 sacks/ha rent at R$119.52/sack, or R$1,792.80/ha; total cost was R$6,817.62/ha for the tenant versus R$5,024.82/ha for the owner, and break-even was 57.04 versus 42.04 sacks/ha. Figures are a July 2026 producer-association cost model, not every farmer's actual contract. (read) [Source: https://aprosojams.org.br/sites/default/files/estudos/Estudo%20T%C3%A9cnico%20custo%20producao%20soja%2026%2027.pdf]
**Who carries the cost.** Tenant pays rent and crop risk; owner receives rent but bears land and counterparty risk; lender sees rent as a prior claim on harvest revenue. (read)
**Counterexample.** Long leases exist: a July 2026 report described an SLC lease at 19.5 sacks/ha over 15 years. That is evidence that tenant status does not necessarily mean a short horizon, though the company contract is not a market-wide survey. (read) [Source: https://agfeed.com.br/negocios/cosan-divide-terras-da-radar-entre-slc-e-bom-futuro-e-conclui-negocio-de-r-18-bilhao/]

### Fertilizer cost and investment cutback
**Mechanism.** Imported input exposure and the delivered cost of fertilizers influence nutrient rates and cash available for cover crops or diversification. In MT, Conab described a response in which growers protect hectares but optimize or reduce investment. (read) [Source: https://www.gov.br/conab/pt-br/atuacao/logistica/boletim-logistico/boletim-logistico-outubro-2026.pdf]
**Numbers.** Conab recorded 28.46 Mt fertilizer imports in Jan-Sep 2026 against 33.75 Mt in Jan-Sep 2025, down about 15.7% by those totals; the report noted high input prices and lower MT import volume. (read)
**Who carries the cost.** Owner/tenant pays cash or grain barter; input supplier/co-op finances and sells product; lender bears default risk if yield suffers. (read)
**Counterexample.** National import volumes do not identify a given farm's application rate: farmers can draw from stocks, change formulations, or adjust nutrient timing. Lower imports are a market signal, not proof that every field reduced fertilizer. (read)

### Freight to port and basis
**Mechanism.** A port price is not a farm-gate price. Road freight, terminal, mode and route change netback and hence the relative attractiveness of soy, maize or alternative crops. (read) [Source: https://www.gov.br/conab/pt-br/atuacao/logistica/boletim-logistico/boletim-logistico-outubro-2026.pdf]
**Numbers.** Conab's monthly survey for Sep 2026 listed Sorriso-Santos at R$500/t over 1,961 km, Sorriso-Miritituba at R$280/t over 1,076 km, and Sorriso-Paranaguá at R$490/t over 2,128 km. (read)
**Who carries the cost.** Trader/exporter generally handles the port basis and logistics but embeds the freight deduction in the farm-gate offer; tenant and owner ultimately see the net price. (read)
**Counterexample.** There is no single “Mato Grosso freight”: in the same Sep 2026 Conab table, Querência-Santos was R$450/t, Campo Novo do Parecis-Santos R$500/t, and Primavera do Leste-Santos R$410/t. Route and origin matter. (read)

### ILPF capital and return horizon
**Mechanism.** ILPF [integrated crop-livestock-forest] combines crop, livestock and trees, but the tree component adds establishment capital and delayed revenue. Its returns depend on local timber use/markets, pasture recovery, operator skill and access to long-term land and credit. (read) [Source: https://frontiersin.org/journals/sustainable-food-systems/articles/10.3389/fsufs.2025.1518747/full]
**Numbers.** A 2025 study's Nova Xavantina-Cerrado case used 2011-2017 farm data: initial investment US$1,024.30/ha; gross profit US$364.87/ha/year versus US$338.33 for a soy-corn system; IRR 15.78% versus 8.81%; both systems had seven-year payback. The paper expresses its indicators at 2023 prices and gives a 2023 exchange basis of R$5.22/US$. (read)
**Who carries the cost.** Owner or long-tenure tenant pays for capital and delayed tree return; lender must match loan duration; timber buyer and cattle/crop markets provide separate revenue channels. (read)
**Counterexample.** The case-study advantage is modest over its comparator: gross profit was US$26.54/ha/year higher and payback matched the crop system. Results from one successful farm case do not establish average adoption economics for MT, GO, MS or BA. (read)

### Cattle cycle and integration type
**Mechanism.** A large share of “integrated” Brazilian area is crop-livestock, not crop-livestock-forest. For Cerrado decisions, beef prices, pasture quality and dry-season feed can dominate; tree planting needs distinct timber use or market. (read) [Source: https://frontiersin.org/journals/sustainable-food-systems/articles/10.3389/fsufs.2025.1518747/full]
**Numbers.** The 2025 paper cites a 2017 Rede ILPF estimate that 83% of integrated area was ILP; in the cited farm economics, cattle gross profit in 2020 was 3.75 times the 2015 level. These are historical figures and do not describe the 2026 cattle cycle. (read)
**Who carries the cost.** Owner funds pasture/cattle system; cattle buyer and livestock prices drive returns; tenant tenure and loan maturity constrain pasture or tree investments. (read)
**Counterexample.** RS integrated-crop-livestock hectares should not be interpreted as Cerrado ILPF with trees. An “integrated area” total without system modality can mislead the recommendation. (read)

### No-till and the rotation deficit
**Mechanism.** No-till adoption is not equivalent to a complete conservation system: minimum disturbance, permanent cover and crop diversification are separate pillars. In PR/RS the baseline is mature no-till, while the marginal choice is often winter crop, cover crop or terrace maintenance; in Cerrado it is often a second crop or pasture integration. (read) [Source: https://www.embrapa.br/en/busca-de-noticias/-/noticia/149291203/embrapa-solos-celebra-o-dia-nacional-do-plantio-direto]
**Numbers.** Embrapa Solos reported 45.8 Mha under no-till, with 6.4 Mha (14%) meeting all three pillars, based on its 63-farm SPD Agro+ analysis reported 2026-10-26. Embrapa's 2021 PR report said 80% of grain farms used no-till and estimated US$242m/year nutrient losses from low conservation practice; that figure is an estimate attributed to cited studies. (read) [Source: https://www.embrapa.br/busca-de-noticias/-/noticia/66091862/regiao-oeste-do-parana-e-modelo-em-agricultura-conservacionista]
**Who carries the cost.** Owner and tenant pay for cover seed, residue management and terraces; co-op and extension systems affect knowledge and input access; society bears off-farm erosion/nutrient impacts. The 2021 report described C.Vale's role in local technical assistance, an institutional co-op channel with an interest in grain handling, inputs and services. (read)
**Counterexample.** The 14% figure is a three-pillar compliance measure, not a yield ranking or proof that remaining farms are conventionally tilled. A farm can no-till and still lack rotation or permanent cover. (read)

### Winter crop opportunity cost: wheat, maize, canola and barley
**Mechanism.** In PR and RS, winter wheat and alternatives compete for field access, capital and machinery after summer crops. In PR, safrinha maize occupies a major summer follow-on area; in RS, early maize, canola, cover crops and off-season soybean can substitute for wheat. (read) [Sources: https://revistacultivar.com/news/2025-harvest%3A-26-prices-slow-wheat-advance ; https://agricultura.rs.gov.br/inicia-periodo-recomendado-de-plantio-de-trigo-no-rs]
**Numbers.** PR wheat area was reported at 722,000 ha in 2026, down 12% from 820,000 ha in 2025, while PR safrinha maize covered 2.84 Mha in 2026. RS wheat area was 763,121 ha in 2026 versus 1,166,163 ha in 2025, and canola area rose to 379,887 ha in 2026, up 117.83% year on year. (read)
**Who carries the cost.** Owner bears the land opportunity cost; tenant bears delayed cash and lease obligations; co-op/miller or crop buyer may influence the crop mix through local contracts and facilities. (read)
**Counterexample.** Wheat is not automatically displaced: a 2025/26 system may still hold a local contract, crop insurance, or rotation value that the state-level price-area comparison cannot capture. In RS, the same 2026 Emater update shows canola yield was revised down 17.23% to 1,340 kg/ha even as area more than doubled. (read) [Source: https://globorural.globo.com/agricultura/noticia/2026/10/previsao-para-a-produtividade-do-trigo-cresce-no-rs-mas-colheita-deve-cair-quase-40percent-em-2026.ghtml]

### Wheat price-cost squeeze
**Mechanism.** Wheat may serve as a rotation crop or spread fixed costs, but growers compare its expected margin with competing crops and quality/marketing risk. (read) [Source: https://revistacultivar.com/news/2025-harvest%3A-26-prices-slow-wheat-advance]
**Numbers.** Deral's Jan 2026 reference price of R$62.19 per 60-kg sack was close to the estimated 56 sacks/ha production cost and 57 sacks/ha 2025 average yield. The same report placed wheat at 15% above corn, versus a competitive threshold estimate near 80%. (read)
**Who carries the cost.** Owner/tenant pay establishment and harvest costs; lender faces collateral/revenue risk; co-op/miller may benefit from throughput but carries quality-market exposure. (read)
**Counterexample.** CEPEA's Nov 2026 indicator later rose to R$1,555.77/t in PR and R$1,465.99/t in RS, but that later rally does not mean every farmer had a forward contract or that price alone repaired quality and yield losses. (read) [Source: https://cepea.org.br/br/indicador/trigo.aspx]

### Frost, rain and insurance in southern wheat
**Mechanism.** Southern winter-crop timing trades frost exposure at flowering against rain and quality damage at harvest. Insurance and credit eligibility affect whether a farmer accepts that weather risk. (read) [Source: https://valorinternational.globo.com/agribusiness/news/2025/07/08/adverse-weather-hampers-wheat-crop-in-southern-brazil.ghtml]
**Numbers.** PR's 2025 wheat crop assessment reported good fields declining from 99% to 84%, 7% poor and more than 50,000 ha below forecast. In Oct 2026, with 82% harvested, 13% of PR fields were rated poor. Valor reported in Mar 2026 that about 58,000 farmers were excluded from Proagro in 2024/25 and about 55,000 lacked another risk instrument. (read)
**Who carries the cost.** Farmer bears yield, quality and deductible loss; lender bears repayment risk; insurer and government bear contracted/eligible exposure. (read)
**Counterexample.** Frost is not the only hazard: the 2026 report cited rain at harvest and quality loss, so a frost-only calendar or model misses the season's dominant downside in some locations. (read)

### Co-operative relationship and patronage
**Mechanism.** A co-op may provide storage, credit, inputs, technical advice, grain purchase and member rebates, making it a practical extension channel. The same co-op can have a commercial interest in grain throughput, crop-specific processing or input sales; recommendations should name that interest rather than assume neutrality. (read) [Source: https://istoedinheiro.com.br/coamo-teve-em-2025-receita-global-de-r-287-bi-e-sobra-liquida-de-r-2-bi]
**Numbers.** Coamo's 2025 results, reported 2026-02-06, included R$28.7bn revenue, 32,736 members, 9.617 Mt received and a R$1.30/60-kg-sack wheat rebate. Co-op magazine reporting lists 303 agronomists, 38 veterinarians and 15 technicians, approximately 2023. (read)
**Who carries the cost.** Member pays through delivery and purchasing choices; co-op funds facilities/advice and earns from handling, trading and input sales; agronomist operates inside the institution's incentives. (read)
**Counterexample.** Co-ops can enable diversification as well as shape it: storage and grain handling can make wheat/maize feasible, and a delivery/rebate advantage does not establish that wheat is agronomically or financially best for every member. Coamo's own reported figures are interested-source data. (read)

### Barter and input-supplier credit
**Mechanism.** The farmer obtains inputs before planting and repays with grain, often binding a source of inputs and a buyer/originator. That can bridge a controlled-credit gap but reduces choice of supplier or grain outlet. (read) [Source: https://valorinternational.globo.com/agribusiness/news/2025/10/27/input-suppliers-gain-ground-in-brazils-farm-credit-market.ghtml]
**Numbers.** CropLife/CropData figures reported in Oct 2025 put barter credit at R$12.6bn in 2024, 72% above 2022; input suppliers lent R$80.8bn, about one-third of farm operating credit. CropLife represents input-sector interests. (read)
**Who carries the cost.** Farmer pays through implicit financing terms, delivery commitment and lost price optionality; supplier/trader bears counterparty and origination risk while earning on input/grain flows. (read)
**Counterexample.** Barter is not the only path: an Embrapa study of two MT municipalities found bank-financed farmers had more input and sales options than barter farmers. That is local, older survey evidence, not a national causal estimate. (read) [Source: https://rpa.sede.embrapa.br/RPA/article/download/1852/pdf_1/3859]

### Extension, FEBRAPDP and farmer-to-farmer network
**Mechanism.** Brazilian no-till spread through farmer clubs, local foundations, cooperatives and field trials as well as public extension. A US-centric model that assumes a farmer evaluates a digital recommendation alone misses trusted local advisers and peer demonstrations. Historical network accounts have an advocacy perspective and adoption definitions vary. (read) [Source: https://plantiodireto.org.br/uploads/download/publicacoes/Isco_final_Revised__versao_americana_s._figuras_.pdf]
**Numbers.** A historical FEBRAPDP account records Clube Amigos da Terra activity from 1982, 43 clubs and 8.7 Mha no-till by 1997/98. Embrapa Soja and IDR-Paraná planned a 2025/26 technical tour through 14 municipalities, 60-80 participants each and over 1,000 expected. (read)
**Who carries the cost.** Farmer bears adoption and implementation risk; co-op, extension agent and peer network lower information cost; input sponsors may benefit from promoted products and should be disclosed. (read)
**Counterexample.** The 2026 Embrapa Solos report snippet that only 14% met all three no-till pillars suggests network diffusion of a practice label can outpace full-system implementation; the metric is a definition/compliance measure, not an audited yield comparison. (URL verified, not read) [Source: https://embrapa.br/en/busca-de-noticias/-/noticia/149291203/embrapa-solos-celebra-o-dia-nacional-do-plantio-direto]

## Region notes

### Cerrado: MT, GO, MS and BA
The Cerrado decision system is often a high-intensity sequence of soy followed by maize or cotton, with cover/forage alternatives when the second-crop window closes. The strongest scheduling link is soy maturity and harvest date to second-crop planting; the Santa Carmem-MT experiment measured 2.33 Mg/ha difference between 19 January and 27 February maize sowing in a specific 2018 trial, not a state-wide forecast. (read) [Source: https://rbms.sede.embrapa.br/ojs/article/download/1345/1615/11443]

Cotton is a specialized, high-input alternative; cotton's spray burden should be treated separately from maize's leafhopper and stunt risk. MT nematode pressure makes the choice of host/non-host crop matter, while legal reserve and CAR are property-level constraints. For Mato Grosso properties inside the Legal Amazon, the legal reserve minimum depends on the property's biome; it is 35% for Cerrado properties and 80% for forest properties, not a uniform statewide number. (read) [Source: https://www.planalto.gov.br/ccivil_03/_ato2011-2014/2012/lei/l12651.htm]

ILPF in this region can be a pasture-recovery and cattle-intensification strategy, but should not be recommended as if tree returns are immediate. A 2025 Nova Xavantina case study showed a modest gross-profit difference over soy-corn and equal seven-year payback; one farm case does not stand in for the whole of MT, GO, MS or BA. (read) [Source: https://frontiersin.org/journals/sustainable-food-systems/articles/10.3389/fsufs.2025.1518747/full]

### Paraná and Rio Grande do Sul: no-till soy-wheat and competing winter crops
In PR/RS, no-till is a long-established base practice, while winter crop profitability, weather risk, credit and quality markets more often determine the next rotation. PR's 2026 wheat area and safrinha maize area show a strong summer follow-on crop alongside a smaller winter wheat area; RS 2026 wheat area fell while canola expanded. These state totals do not say which individual farms switched crops. (read) [Sources: https://revistacultivar.com/news/2025-harvest%3A-26-prices-slow-wheat-advance ; https://globorural.globo.com/agricultura/noticia/2026/10/previsao-para-a-produtividade-do-trigo-cresce-no-rs-mas-colheita-deve-cair-quase-40percent-em-2026.ghtml]

Disease risk differs from Cerrado: Embrapa tied higher rust reports in the South to wet winter survival of volunteer soy and early sowing, while dry winter in Cerrado reduces volunteer-soy survival. Wheat's window must balance frost and harvest rain, and the Proagro cap/insurance availability are material constraints for some growers. (read) [Sources: https://www.embrapa.br/busca-de-noticias/-/noticia/106270547/clima-e-falhas-em-manejo-explicam-aumento-da-incidencia-de-ferrugem-da-soja-no-parana ; https://valorinternational.globo.com/agribusiness/news/2026/03/31/proagro-changes-cut-access-to-credit-and-crop-protection.ghtml]

**Do not average the regions.** A Cerrado tool should model the safrinha cutoff, crop overlap, freight, nematodes, cotton investment and reserve status. A PR/RS tool should model winter wheat economics, frost/rain timing, co-op/miller channels, no-till cover/rotation completeness and volunteer-soy rust exposure. A single Brazil-wide “rotation” score will hide the actual decision.

**Where Brazilian practice most breaks a US-centric tool's assumptions.** First, the crop calendar is sequential: soybean cultivar maturity sets whether a profitable safrinha crop can be planted before the local dry-season/ZARC limit, rather than a simple annual crop rotation. Second, finance and agronomy are joined: Plano Safra availability, Proagro/PSR eligibility and ZARC compliance can rule a technically viable option in or out. Third, the sales price is a farm-gate netback shaped by local freight routes and barter/co-op delivery terms, not a single nearby elevator basis. Fourth, the legal field unit is nested in CAR boundaries, biome-specific legal reserve and property history; a uniform acreage constraint misses this. Fifth, extension and coordination often run through co-ops and farmer networks with input, grain-handling or processing interests, not only independent advisers. The southern system adds a different break: mature no-till and winter wheat/frost/rain economics, which cannot be inferred from Cerrado soy-maize-cotton rotations. (read)

## Second crop timing

The sequence is: soy sow date and cultivar maturity group -> soy harvest date -> time and equipment available for second-crop sowing -> flowering and grain fill relative to rainfall/ZARC -> financing and insurance eligibility. (read) [Source: https://rbms.sede.embrapa.br/ojs/article/download/1345/1615/11443]

In the Cerrado-Amazon transition, a 2024 Embrapa/UFMT field paper states second-crop maize should be sown by approximately 20 February to avoid reproductive-stage water shortage; its 2018 Santa Carmem experiment measured 2.33 Mg/ha more yield from 19 January than 27 February sowing. Treat this as location-specific evidence rather than an identical cutoff for all of MT, GO, MS or BA. (read)

Soy maturity group sets the window because a longer or later-maturing soy cultivar can delay harvest. In Dois Vizinhos-PR's 2016/17 and 2017/18 field data, MG 6.0 sown mid-September allowed maize planting in mid-February, while soy sown 15 October made the second maize crop uneconomic regardless of maturity group. The trial's South-specific GMs should not be transplanted to Cerrado recommendations. (read) [Source: https://www.scielo.br/j/resr/a/Cp5dGMsPtgBh4G3Mz48SzFs?lang=en]

A farmer with a late soy harvest is not automatically making a bad decision by leaving maize out. Aprosoja-MT's 2024 guide describes sorghum, millet, sesame, sunflower, brachiaria and crotalaria options when late maize becomes uncertain; those alternatives trade grain income, biomass, pest breaks, water use and market access. In 2025-2026, ZARC observance also became a condition of agricultural custeio credit, so the tool must use the applicable municipality/soil/cultivar window and financing channel, not a fixed national date. (read) [Sources: https://siteapi.aprosoja.com.br/storage/arquivos/4/240724142657.pdf ; https://normativos.bcb.gov.br/Votos/CMN/202535/Voto_do_CMN_35_2025.pdf]

## What I could not find

- A farm-level panel that estimates how rotation choices differ between owner-operators and tenants, or a direct causal estimate of lease duration on cover-crop, nematode-break or ILPF adoption. The tenant cost mechanism is documented; its causal effect is not.
- A 2025/26 census of ILPF by state and modality. The 83% ILP figure is a 2017 estimate cited in a 2025 paper; the economic result is a small number of case studies.
- An official farm-level series of delivered fertilizer prices and application rates by nutrient for MT/GO/MS/BA in 2026. Conab import tonnage and market commentary do not measure a particular farm's kg/ha.
- A public, complete panel of ZARC exceptions and crop-credit denials by municipality, soil class and crop for the new all-custeio rule.
- A current, independent state-by-state dataset of soybean rust losses; the Embrapa 2026 record count is not a loss estimate and varies with reporting intensity.
- A national current estimate of Cercospora-specific yield losses or soybean fungicide applications per season. MS trial results are local and disease-specific.

- A 2026 EUDR share of Brazilian soybean volume by destination and a verified estimate of compliance cost per farm or tonne.

- Bahia-specific farm-level cotton rotation, nematode and insurance data comparable to the MT/PR materials.
