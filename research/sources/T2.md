# T2 Non-NASA soil and crop data sources

**Status key:** `(read)` means the primary source page, metadata, service or live response was read; `(URL verified, not read)` means only the URL/endpoint was checked; `(discovery only)` means found from a search result or secondary reference and not verified from the primary source. Interpretive recommendations are marked `(read, inference)`. Findings are current to 2026-11-10 where current status was tested. Native map scale is not a cell size or a claim of positional accuracy. ## Per source

### gSSURGO
NRCS surveyors map soil map units from field observations, transects, sampling and laboratory analyses; the downloadable database links each polygon/map-unit key to component, horizon and interpretation tables. The grid is a rendering of map-unit boundaries, so many adjacent 10 m or 30 m cells repeat the same soil unit. The aggregation trap is treating those repeated cells as independent observations or treating a map unit as homogeneous; components and inclusions need an explicit, component-proportion-weighted rule. Join cost to a 30 m satellite grid is low computationally, but CONUS downloads are large and split across files; rasterize/intersect by MUKEY and retain survey-area provenance. (read)

### gNATSGO
This is a composite national database: detailed SSURGO where available, generalized STATSGO2 where detailed coverage is missing, and Raster Soil Surveys in some areas. The national seamless layer mixes source scales and evidence quality; carrying a 30 m cell size into a display does not make generalized areas field-scale. Keep provenance/source class and suppress field-level precision where the base map is generalized. Join cost is low once the national GeoPackage and rasters are downloaded, but storage and unpacking are nontrivial. (read)

### Web Soil Survey
The user selects or uploads an area of interest and asks NRCS to render mapped soil units, interpretations and printable reports from survey records. The trap is assuming the output is an onsite soil diagnosis: map-unit components, chosen depth and interpretation/aggregation method change the result, and onsite investigation may still be required. No automated 30 m join is needed for manual review; for an app, WSS is an analyst verification route, not a stable bulk ingest API. (read)

### Cropland Data Layer
The CDL is a remotely sensed annual crop/land-cover classification, using satellite imagery and agricultural reference information; NASS metadata documents its inputs and class-specific accuracy caveats. Pixel counts are not identical to official acreage estimates; class errors, mixed pixels and a changed 2024+ 10 m workflow make cross-vintage comparisons require care. Double-cropped areas can appear as combined classes, and a single annual class does not encode all management operations. Join cost is low: clip/reproject the raster to the satellite grid, preserve original class and year, and do not imply 10 m history before 2024. (read)

### CroplandCROS
CroplandCROS is an interactive viewer and analysis/export front end for CDL rather than an independent crop survey. Its area statistics are derived from classified pixels and inherit the CDL's classification/acreage limitations; the single time slider spans years with different native resolutions. Join cost is nearly zero for an individual AOI, but bulk repeatable application joins should use CDL data products directly; anonymous export was not verified. (read)

### NASS Quick Stats
Quick Stats combines sample survey estimates and Census results, indexed by crop, year, geography and statistic. County crop estimates are statistical aggregates, not field observations; estimates can be modeled, withheld or unavailable for some crops/states. Joining a field to its county is cheap but introduces ecological fallacy: a county mean does not establish a field's yield or management. The API is free but requires emailed key registration, so it is identity-gated. (read)

### RUSLE2
RUSLE2 is a user-run erosion model, not an observed erosion raster. It combines climate inputs, SSURGO-derived soil, management templates and user-specified slope length/gradient to estimate long-term average rill/interrill soil loss. Its output is for a representative profile and does not describe every point, gully erosion or wind erosion; a scenario is not an observed management history. Joining to a 30 m grid is costly and misleading unless the app can run and calibrate field-specific slope/soil/management inputs; there is no web API or Linux build. (read)

### PRISM
PRISM interpolates station observations using topographic and other predictors to create gridded climate estimates and long-term normals. The 4 km or 800 m grid is modeled/interpolated, not a weather station at each cell; 30-year normals smooth extremes, and PRISM notes limitations for long-term trend analysis. Reproject and sample/aggregate to a 30 m grid only as a climate covariate; nearest-neighbour replication would falsely imply 30 m climate information. Join cost is low using point series or grid downloads, with citation conditions. (read)

### Colorado CDSS / HydroBase
The state system exposes administrative water rights, diversion structures, streams, irrigated lands and related records via tools and REST services. A decree's priority and diversion rate are not equivalent to measured delivery or field application; point structures are not field boundaries. Spatially joining rights to an irrigated field requires point/polygon matching and local legal interpretation. Join cost is medium because service records and field geometry must be reconciled; other states have different systems and definitions. (read)

### USDA AMS My Market News / MARS
AMS market reporters gather bids/trades from voluntary industry reporting, including phone, email and company websites, with verification/periodic visits described in the programme material. This is a sampled set of reported market quotes, not every buyer or a farmgate price; reports can exclude insignificant/outlier data and typically quote an elevator/grade/basis. Joining requires market report selection, location matching and distance/basis assumptions, not raster resampling. MARS API key access is gated even though reports are publicly viewable. (read)

### IBGE Pedologia 1:250,000
IBGE publishes mapped soil units as vector and sheet products compiled from surveys and classifications. A polygon represents a map unit, often a soil association, not a measured uniform field soil; 1:250,000 is the map scale, not a 250 m pixel or a 10 m soil map. For 30 m imagery, intersection creates many repeated map-unit labels but adds no detail; use only as a regional prior, and record sheet/vintage. (read)

### Embrapa GeoInfo Brazil map
The national Embrapa GeoInfo soil map is a broad 1:5,000,000 compilation. The layer identifier's `5m` is a scale shorthand, not a five-metre raster; its 2,852 polygons and SiBCS classes cannot support farm-scale soil assignments. Resampling to a 30 m grid only duplicates broad polygons. The catalogue record states CC BY-NC, a blocker for commercial redistribution absent separate permission. (read)

### Embrapa BDsolos / Redape
BDsolos aggregates laboratory profile and horizon records collected by different projects and laboratories over time; its public catalogue redirects to the Redape repository. It is sample-based and spatially uneven, with method, depth and analytical metadata varying among records. Do not interpolate profiles as if they were a probability sample or a uniform national survey. Joining profiles to satellite pixels is useful for local calibration/validation only after coordinate, depth and method harmonization. The CC BY-NC terms constrain commercial reuse. (read)

### PronaSolos / SGB 90 m attributes
The 90 m service exposes soil attributes, including modelled carbon stocks by depth, rather than direct measurement in every grid cell. It draws on legacy point data and digital soil mapping/interpolation; sparse and uneven profile locations and model uncertainty are the aggregation trap. A 90 m grid is more useful than a national-scale polygon map, but it remains 3 by 3 nominal 30 m pixels and must not be called a 30 m soil survey. Sampling to the satellite grid is computationally cheap; attribution, model uncertainty and unclear service reuse terms are the real costs. (read)

### PronaSolos portal
PronaSolos is a programme and heterogeneous catalogue, not one national map product. State layers have different vintages, scales and source methods; some are 1:250,000, which remains a regional survey product rather than field-scale 10 m SSURGO-like detail. The trap is advertising a portal's finest listed scale as uniform national coverage. Before joining, inspect each layer's metadata, service source and licence. (read)

### MapBiomas Collection 11
MapBiomas classifies land cover/use from satellite image time series and publishes annual 30 m maps. Reprocessing across collections can change historical labels, so differences between collection versions are not necessarily real land change; class accuracy also differs by biome and class. Agropecuária categories do not mean an exact crop identity for every year/field. Join with 30 m pixels directly but keep collection number and class confidence/limitations. CC BY-SA terms affect derivatives and redistribution. (read)

### MapBiomas Soil Collection 3
The product uses a training repository of soil samples, predictive covariates and machine-learning/pedotransfer methods to estimate annual soil organic carbon and static soil attributes. Missing values are estimated, so pixels are modeled, not sampled. The source documents an artificial 2021–2022 carbon-series drop; do not treat that change as a field soil-carbon event. Native 30 m raster aligns conveniently with satellite imagery but shares no independent 30 m measurement support. Keep collection and model vintage; CC BY-SA applies. (read)

### PRODES
PRODES maps annual increments of native-vegetation suppression from satellite imagery using biome-specific protocols. It reports mapped increments, not all land-cover transitions or a crop rotation; minimum mapping area and sensor/resolution differ by biome and vintage. Treat an annual polygon as a contextual land-conversion alert, not field crop history. Vector intersection with fields is straightforward, but the annual window, biome protocol and no-explicit-licence finding must travel with output. (read)

### DETER
DETER is a frequent alert system for suspected vegetation suppression/degradation using satellite sensors and validation, designed to support enforcement, not to estimate annual deforestation rates. Cloud, alert thresholds, sensor changes and documentation differences matter; primary sources checked disagree on a 3 ha versus 6.25 ha minimum, which remains unresolved. An alert polygon near a field is a warning for follow-up, not confirmed cleared area or crop identity. Spatial join is easy; interpretation cost is high. (read)

### SICAR / CAR
CAR property boundaries and environmental declarations are submitted by registrants and are subject to later state-level validation. They are administrative self-declarations, not surveyed cadastral boundaries; overlaps, errors and pending analyses matter. The public consultation portal was inaccessible to automation during this check and no open federal bulk API was found. Do not imply a complete, redistributable national field-boundary layer; joining available polygons to 30 m pixels is easy technically but carries substantial validation and political/privacy risk. (read)

### ZARC / SISZARC
ZARC portarias combine crop, municipality, planting decêndio, risk level, cultivar group and soil class. They are administrative agronomic risk recommendations tied to insurance/credit instruments, not parcel-level measured conditions. Soil grouping changed: older rules may use three texture classes while newer water-availability methodology uses six classes; mixing editions changes recommendations. Portarias are PDFs and SISZARC is the public query route; no machine API was found. Geocoding a field to municipality is easy, but checking the correct season, cultivar and governing portaria is essential. (read)

### MAPA RNC / CultivarWeb
CultivarWeb records official registration status and cultivar identity through a registry form. Registration is not a performance trial or a recommendation for a given soil/climate; names and maintainers can change. The form exposed CAPTCHA and no bulk export/API was established, making repeatable joins costly. Normalize cultivar identifiers carefully and keep the registry date. (read)

### Embrapa cultivar and forage catalogues
Embrapa web catalogue pages describe cultivars/species and traits for its own material, including forage species pages. They are not a full market catalogue, standardized trial database or spatial suitability surface; missing entries do not mean a crop is unavailable. One forage index link was broken during checking, while per-species pages resolved. Use as discovery material, then validate seed availability, cultivar registration and local performance; no 30 m join is appropriate. (read)

### CONAB historical prices
CONAB panels compile prices from CONAB and partners at particular commodities and market levels, with weekly/monthly and historical series depending on panel. They are market series, not a farmgate quote for a specific field. Match product, market level, location and date; do not treat a national/wholesale series as the farmer's realized price. The page expressly permits reproduction only for non-commercial purposes with attribution and integrity, so commercial redistribution requires permission. The public portal provides dashboards, but a stable API/bulk endpoint was not verified. (read)

### Agrotools
Agrotools describes a commercial agricultural supply-chain/compliance data platform; its coverage statements are vendor claims, not independent validation. It is a paid/proprietary alternative, with public pages not disclosing pricing or a blanket redistribution right. Do not assume that buying access includes model-output redistribution. No grid resolution was stated in reviewed public material; field join cost and data provenance require vendor diligence. (read)

### STATSGO2
STATSGO2 is a generalized soil map compiled from detailed surveys and, where those are absent, broader landscape relationships and ancillary data. Its map units represent associations with high/low/representative ranges, and Alaska is mapped at 1:1,000,000 while much of CONUS is 1:250,000. Do not rasterize and present as field detail. Join cost to a 30 m grid is low computationally, but the scale mismatch makes it only a regional fallback. (read)

### Crop Sequence Boundaries
CSB polygons are algorithmically synthesized representations of agricultural fields from CDL time series and ancillary road/rail information; they are not cadastral boundaries. CDL class error propagates into both boundary placement and attached sequence, and the 2022 metadata's use-constraint field is blank. Joining CDL class history by polygon is straightforward, but field realism and licence ambiguity are the traps. (read)

### Crop Frequency Layers
The 2025 layer counts the number of years each included crop appears in a fixed historical window, resampled to 30 m for consistency. Frequency does not preserve order, double-crop detail or complete rotation chronology. Join cost is low because it is already raster; its historical window and intentional downsampling must be stated. (read)

### Census of Agriculture
NASS Census is a five-year complete count of farms meeting the sales threshold, released only as protected aggregates. It is administrative/statistical county data, not a sample at field level; county suppression and the 2022-to-2026 vintage gap are aggregation traps. The county join is cheap, but individual-farm management cannot be inferred. (read)

## Brazilian specifics

- **Soil-map scarcity:** Brazil does not have a public, nationwide, 10 m equivalent to US SSURGO. The best readily discoverable national soil map is IBGE at 1:250,000 in relevant sheet coverage, plus the Embrapa national compilation at 1:5,000,000 and modeled 90 m attribute products. A 1:250,000 map is a distinct regional map product, not a 10 m SSURGO map unit; a finer raster posting does not create denser observations. Less than 5% of Brazil was reported mapped at 1:100,000 or finer in the federal PronaSolos programme summary ([official federal summary](https://www.gov.br/en/government-of-brazil/latest-news/2022/pronasolos-platform-allow-access-to-all-who-produce-soil-data-in-brazil)) (read).
- **Cerrado:** Coverage and map scale are heterogeneous by state/project; national compilation and regional survey layers must not be conflated with detailed field mapping. No verified public Cerrado-wide 10 m soil-unit product was found. The SGB/PronaSolos service catalogue should be checked per layer for exact footprint, map scale, vintage and licence. (read)
- **PronaSolos:** It is an ongoing national soil-survey programme and data portal, not evidence that detailed national mapping is already complete. Public products include regional/state surveys and modelled national 90 m attributes; do not state its target scale as achieved coverage. The federal programme summary describes a long-term mapping target, while individual layer coverage and release cadence vary. (read)
- **SICAR/CAR:** CAR is self-declared rural-property and environmental-registration information subject to state validation. The public consultation was denied to this automated session; no open national bulk API or blanket redistribution permission was verified. It is politically and legally sensitive: never represent submitted boundaries as surveyed property lines, validated legal reserves or a clean field-boundary truth layer. (read)
- **ZARC:** Official crop-risk zoning is issued by crop/state/season and can influence agricultural insurance support and credit. It is politically consequential; soil classes and methodologies changed, so keep the exact portaria and season with every result. There is no verified public machine API; use official portarias/SISZARC rather than reverse-engineering PDFs into an unlabelled authoritative map. (read)
- **MapBiomas:** Collection 11 land cover provides annual 30 m mapping through 2025; Collection 3 Soil has annual modeled SOC through 2024. Reprocessed collections can change prior years, MapBiomas Soil documents a 2021–2022 SOC artifact, and CC BY-SA imposes share-alike obligations. These products are useful regional priors and change-history layers, not field soil surveys. (read)
- **PRODES and DETER:** PRODES is annual suppression mapping, while DETER is a frequent alert stream; DETER is not a deforestation rate. They have sensor, minimum-area, cloud and biome-protocol differences. No explicit dataset redistribution licence was found on the examined download pages, so cite INPE and verify terms for product redistribution. DETER's checked pages disagree on minimum area, which remains unresolved. (read)
- **Licensing and political sensitivity:** Embrapa GeoInfo's national soil map and BDsolos/Redape records carry non-commercial Creative Commons terms; MapBiomas uses CC BY-SA; CONAB historical prices permit reproduction only for non-commercial purposes; the SGB 90 m service did not expose a licence field. CAR geometry and ZARC recommendations are legally/politically consequential even when viewable. Commercial product use requires permission or a different source where terms are non-commercial/unclear. (read)
- **Cultivars and forage:** MAPA RNC/CultivarWeb gives registration, not local performance. Embrapa pages cover Embrapa material only and some catalogue access is web-page based; neither provides a complete, machine-ready seed market or location-specific trial dataset. (read)
- **Market:** CONAB provides public agricultural prices with a non-commercial reproduction condition. It is a viable public research source, but not a commercial app's redistributable price layer absent a separate licence. Agrotools is a commercial/proprietary alternative; pricing and output redistribution terms were not public in material reviewed. (read)

## US specifics

- **gSSURGO and gNATSGO:** gSSURGO offers detailed mapped survey units and pre-summarized attributes, but its raster cells repeat map-unit polygons. gNATSGO provides national continuity by filling gaps with less detailed STATSGO2/Raster Soil Surveys. The 30 m national raster is not uniformly 30 m source information. Preserve source scale and map-unit component proportions. (read)
- **Web Soil Survey:** Best public human-in-the-loop route to inspect a field's survey area, map units, interpretations and printable reports. It is not a bulk field-data API or onsite sampling substitute. (read)
- **CDL and CroplandCROS:** CDL is the annual crop identity layer; CroplandCROS is its viewer/export and area-statistics front end. CDL is 30 m before 2024 and 10 m from 2024; crop year N generally arrives late February of N+1. The history has classification error, double-crop combinations and limitations for pasture/grass; pixel acreage can differ from official statistics. (read)
- **NASS statistics:** Quick Stats offers crop, management and market benchmarks but needs a registered API key, reports county/statistical aggregates and has withheld/modelled values and crop/state coverage limits. The Census of Agriculture is five-yearly (2022 current in this check), reports only aggregates, and cannot reveal a farm's individual management. (read)
- **RUSLE2 and erosion models:** RUSLE2 is a scenario model requiring soil, climate, slope and management inputs; it is not a ready erosion raster. The official software is Windows-only, has no web API, and its published build is old relative to this research date. Treat model output as scenario evidence, not measured field erosion. (read)
- **Climate:** PRISM's current 1991–2020 normals and gridded time series are useful for planting windows; PRISM requires attribution and cautions against some long-term trend uses. (read)
- **State water allocation:** Water allocation is state-specific, not one national data product. Colorado CDSS/HydroBase provides public rights/diversion tools and REST access; it supplies administrative rights and records, not guaranteed delivered volumes. Other states require their own portals, rights definitions and access/licence checks. (read)
- **Market:** USDA AMS My Market News provides local elevator/terminal bids, but API keys require eAuth and reports are voluntary. It is a sampled market signal and not farmgate realized revenue. (read)
