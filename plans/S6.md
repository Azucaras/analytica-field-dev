### S6.1 Policy and credit: glyphosate, the farm bill, Plano Safra
- **question:** As of the November 2026 evidence cutoff, which policy and credit events are already in force, which remain proposals or open reviews, and what can they actually change in a rotation decision?
- **reader:** Farmers, lenders and app designers who need policy signals dated and separated from legal changes or individual loan approvals.
- **entry:** The reader needs to decide whether current weed-control options, US safety-net expectations or Brazilian seasonal credit can be assumed when comparing next-crop alternatives.
- **point:** A policy announcement is not a label change, a proposed farm bill is not enacted law, and a seasonal credit envelope is not a farmer's approved loan; recommendations must distinguish each status and date.
- **chain:** In February 2026, EO 14387 was signed on 2026-02-18 and published 2026-02-23; its operative authority concerns DPA supply priority/allocation and does not impose a glyphosate use restriction. In August 2026, EPA published an open-literature search on 2026-08-25 and said the human-health risk assessment was scheduled for completion in late 2026; that target was scheduled, not a completed assessment or decision. In September 2026, HHS and EPA announced an MOU on 2026-09-22; as of 2026-11-10 no final EPA registration review decision was found, and current labels remained operative. In November 2025, the 2018 US farm bill was extended through FY2026; in April 2026 the House passed H.R. 7567 and in September 2026 the Senate committee advanced its proposal, but no replacement was enacted by the 2026-11 cutoff. In June 2026, MAPA announced R$525.1bn commercial Plano Safra 2026/27, effective 2026-07-01, with controlled-rate examples; that scheduled annual plan coexists with reported July–October disbursements and does not guarantee a drawdown. Satellite data may help observe crop/weed-cover conditions, but cannot settle a pesticide label, legislative enactment or a lender's approval.
- **principle:** Attach every policy claim to an event month and legal status (`settled`, `proposed`, `under review`, `scheduled`); let a qualified source or institution confirm current labels, law and the applicant's credit terms.
- **instance:** Compare the February 2026 supply order with the separate EPA review still open at the November 2026 cutoff, the November 2025 farm-bill extension and un-enacted 2026 proposals, and the July 2026 effective Plano Safra rates. State explicitly that the farm bill and EPA decision were not settled at cutoff, while the plan's announced effective date had passed.
- **limit:** The farm-bill state and floor-status timeline has discovery-only support in T5 in places; do not overstate procedural detail. No final EPA decision was located by 2026-11-10. Planned Plano Safra funds and national rates do not establish an individual's eligibility or access. No satellite product adjudicates policy status.
- **budget:** 600 words.
- **boundary:** This subsection owns policy instruments, legal status and credit-program framing. S6.2 owns dated input prices and crop arithmetic; S6.3 owns climate and pest conditions. S5 owns structural mechanisms and farmer constraints, not repeated chronology.

### S6.2 Input prices and the second-crop arithmetic
- **question:** How do observed input costs, prices and credit availability change the arithmetic of a next crop or safrinha without turning a dated market snapshot into a universal margin forecast?
- **reader:** US and Brazilian growers, agronomists and app builders who need an explicit distinction among observed price, scheduled price input, forecast and farm-specific cost.
- **entry:** A producer compares the incremental costs and possible receipts of corn, soy, cover or a second crop while deciding whether the season's financing and input quotes leave the sequence viable.
- **point:** Second-crop arithmetic is local and dated: fertilizer, fuel, crop prices, freight, credit and the remaining planting window all matter, while national or state averages cannot resolve a particular farm's net return.
- **chain:** In October 2026, USDA AMS's Illinois report for the week ending 2026-10-02 recorded anhydrous ammonia at $945/ton and farm diesel at $5.63/gal; the November 2026 $1,007/ton anhydrous figure in a secondary DTN item is discovery-only and must not be presented as equally verified. In November 2026, WASDE estimates issued 2026-11-10 put US corn at $4.60/bu and soy at $12.10/bu, with crop size and prices still forecast, not final observed outcomes. In Brazil, the July–October 2026 rural-credit release total was R$107.6bn, down 25.7% year-on-year, including nearly R$20bn of renegotiation; September 2026 Conab freight survey quotes and a specific farm's delivery distance affect the netback. A 2024 paper on the 2018 Santa Carmem-MT field experiment described an approximately 20 February practical second-crop sowing bound for that setting, and reported a 2.33 Mg/ha yield difference between 19 January and 27 February sowing. Calendar, financing and the grower's actual invoices must be applied together. Satellite data can characterize crop stage and historical weather/cover, not quote fertilizer, confirm cash credit or settle a freight contract.
- **principle:** Show date, unit, geography, evidence basis, crop-calendar assumption and whether each price is observed, forecast or a local quote; calculate margins only from user-confirmed bids, yields, input rates and financing.
- **instance:** Pair October 2026 AMS input observations with November 2026 WASDE estimates and the Brazil July–October credit report to show that market signals have different dates and states. Use the Santa Carmem local trial as a timing-and-yield example, clearly mark 2018 trial year and 2024 publication, and do not imply its yield gap predicts a 2026/27 farm.
- **limit:** AMS Illinois prices are not a Brazilian or US farm's invoice; November WASDE values remain estimates. T5's November 2026 fertilizer value is discovery-only. Rural-credit disbursement includes restructuring and is not all new cash. The 2018 field trial is site-specific; no region-wide second-crop margin or universal cutoff follows. Remote sensing cannot settle co-op, lender or supplier terms.
- **budget:** 600 words.
- **boundary:** This subsection owns dated commodity/input indicators and their translation into crop-sequence arithmetic. S6.1 owns policy status; S6.3 owns climatic and pest pressure. S4 and S5 own detailed structural economics and locally confirmed contract terms.

### S6.3 Climate and pest pressure through 2026
- **question:** What climate and pest signals were documented by November 2026, what remains a forecast, and how should a rotation tool keep that evidence from becoming a false certainty?
- **reader:** Growers and app teams interpreting climate outlooks, drought indicators and pest reports for upcoming US and Brazilian crop windows.
- **entry:** A user sees an El Niño advisory, a dated US drought footprint and reports about Brazilian safrinha pests, then asks whether those signals establish a crop loss or determine next season's planting choice.
- **point:** As of November 2026, climate and pest evidence can indicate changing exposure, but forecasts, snapshots and retrospective surveys are not observed local yield losses or a substitute for field scouting.
- **chain:** In late 2025, ONI showed weak La Niña; by May 2026 it had transitioned toward El Niño. In October 2026, CPC issued an El Niño Advisory with Niño-3.4 at +2.1°C and an 83% chance of OND RONI at or above +2.5°C; this is an outlook, not the observed outcome of the coming season. In August 2026, NCEI's July drought report measured D0–D4 over 71.0% of CONUS and D1–D4 over 48.5% for the week ending 2026-08-04; the report does not establish November drought conditions. In July 2026, an Aprosoja-MS survey later reported in November found medium/high *Dalbulus maidis* incidence on 14% of properties in 2025/26, down from 41% in 2024/25; the October 2026 alert concerned coming safrinha risk, not a measured El Niño-caused loss. On 2026-11-10, crop-size and price estimates remained provisional; only earlier NASS acreage/stocks observations were settled in the cited data vintage. Satellite observations can support dated rainfall/vegetation context subject to product status, but not diagnose a pest or identify farmer-level crop damage.
- **principle:** Date every climate and pest claim by observation or publication month, label forecasts separately from observations, distinguish sampled incidence from prevalence and yield loss, and call for local scouting before a recommendation.
- **instance:** Put October 2026 CPC forecast alongside August 2026 drought snapshot, and place the July 2026 MS pest survey beside the October 2026 coming-season alert. Mark November 2026 WASDE as forecast and June 2026 NASS planted acreage as observed. Do not attribute pest outcomes to El Niño without evidence.
- **limit:** ENSO is a large-scale advisory and cannot establish a field's rainfall. The drought report ends at 2026-08-04; do not state November drought status from it. The MS pest records are discovery-only in T5 and cover surveyed properties, while the next-season alert is a forecast. Satellite product continuity and scale limit claims: IMERG V08 had not been confirmed released by 2026-11-10; Terra's planned science end date conflicts between February 2027 and 2027-03-01; GRACE-FO's current mode had not been independently refreshed. Do not call data gap a weather fact.
- **budget:** 600 words.
- **boundary:** This subsection owns climate, drought and pest signals through the November 2026 evidence cutoff, including product caveats relevant to those claims. S6.1 owns policy decisions and S6.2 owns price/cost arithmetic; S4.4 and S5.1 own local water and crop-calendar constraints.
