### S7.1 What the companion site exposes to a stranger
- **question:** What can an unauthenticated visitor discover, test, and understand about the Field Shift challenge before building anything?
- **reader:** A small product team that has not visited the challenge companion and needs to distinguish its public guidance from official NASA material.
- **entry:** A public site map of 22 logged-out URLs, with the Field Shift page, dataset cards, access badges, first-hour workflow, starter code, setup guide, and read-only AI/MCP page inspected.
- **point:** The companion is a challenge-first research and onboarding guide, not NASA's data catalogue, submission portal, or proof that a farm decision tool already exists.
- **chain:** Establish the public, no-login routes; describe the six ranked dataset suggestions and separate POWER's no-login access from the five Earthdata Login download routes; trace the first-hour recipe from POWER coordinates through a common grid to three rotation comparisons; close by stating that the Atlas reports no scoreboard or submission flow and identifies itself as independent.
- **principle:** Treat a companion's suggested workflow as a useful starting hypothesis, preserve its provenance and access warnings, and verify challenge requirements against official sources before presenting them as binding.
- **instance:** The Field Shift page says to start with POWER, add SMAP, ECOSTRESS, and GPM on a common grid, then compare at least three rotations including the current one with evidence and risks; the site labels the list likely useful rather than official.
- **limit:** Logged-out inspection cannot rule out private event systems or prove that a suggested product is agronomically suitable for a particular crop, field, or region.
- **budget:** 560 words.
- **boundary:** This subsection owns the companion's public information architecture and stated recipe; S7.2 owns what the code was actually tested to do, while S7.3 owns commercial and public product comparisons.
- **source:** `research/site-prior-art/T6.md`, lines 5-37, 39-49, 112-119, 124-138, 158-170; use the logged-out scope and read-status labels when qualifying claims.
- **file:** `outputs/sections/subsections/S7.1.md`

### S7.2 What the starter code does and does not prove
- **question:** Which parts of the proposed workflow have been reproduced, and which require additional data access, geospatial processing, or agronomic validation?
- **reader:** A developer who can run a NASA catalogue query but may mistake a successful request for an end-to-end science result.
- **entry:** T6 reports seven snippet checks in a named environment: six Python examples using `earthaccess 0.19.0` and `requests`, plus a JavaScript POWER example under Node 26.12.0.
- **point:** The reported tests establish that catalogue searches and small POWER requests ran, not that satellite science files were downloaded, decoded, aligned, classified, or used to score rotations.
- **chain:** Separate metadata search from authenticated download; report the recorded search counts and the POWER example's 153 days and 621.8 mm for its stated 2025 interval; identify the live CMR widget as a five-granule listing, not an HDF5 reader; enumerate missing field geometry, time window, reprojection, masking, quality handling, crop-history classification, and decision validation.
- **principle:** A successful API call proves only the code path it exercised; state the tested layer and leave downstream claims unverified until the actual files and outputs are checked.
- **instance:** T6 says all seven snippets completed in the tested environment and the live CMR request returned HTTP 200 with five records, while Earthdata downloads were not attempted because credentials were unavailable.
- **limit:** These are dated reproducibility checks in T6, not a current rerun in this plan task, and they provide no evidence for agronomic correctness, download authorization, or coverage in a selected field.
- **budget:** 560 words.
- **boundary:** This subsection owns the evidence boundary around starter execution; S7.1 owns the site's user-facing promises, and S9.1 owns a planned, explicitly unverified ingestion and common-grid workflow.
- **source:** `research/site-prior-art/T6.md`, lines 51-63 and 114-122; cite the environment, exact returned values, and account limitation as the pack reports them.
- **file:** `outputs/sections/subsections/S7.2.md`

### S7.3 What existing products already own, and the open gap
- **question:** Which elements of field mapping, farm records, rotation comparison, and rotation scoring are already documented in products, and what combination remains a plausible opening?
- **reader:** A product team deciding whether to build a focused rotation comparison rather than another generic farm dashboard.
- **entry:** T6's read-status-qualified prior-art table covers farm management, satellite monitoring, MRV, rotation records, and whole-rotation scenario tools.
- **point:** Rotation features already exist, so the defensible opportunity is a specific combination of open entry, farmer-correctable observed field history, transparent evidence and uncertainty, and comparison against local constraints and farmer priorities.
- **chain:** Group products by documented job rather than marketing category; distinguish OneSoil's entered rotation records and allocation from image-derived history; distinguish Agreena's vendor-described five-year diversity score from a published, independently validated formula; contrast ADAS and RotationARM modeled comparisons with the pack's finding that their reviewed descriptions did not show satellite-derived field history; describe the gap as an inference at this intersection.
- **principle:** Describe only documented product behavior, identify whether a feature is vendor-reported or independently tested, and call an unoccupied intersection an inference rather than claiming that no competitor does rotation planning.
- **instance:** The pack documents field-history or rotation functions in OneSoil and Agreena, and rotation comparisons in ADAS Crop Planner and RotationARM; it finds no documented match for the complete open, auditable, observed-history and priority-sensitive combination.
- **limit:** Public-page review does not prove that undocumented product functions are absent, and the pack does not independently validate vendor scores, customer outcomes, or agronomic performance.
- **budget:** 560 words.
- **boundary:** This subsection owns existing-product scope and the qualified market gap; S7.1 owns the challenge site's recipe, and S8 owns design principles for the interaction layer rather than competitor feature inventory.
- **source:** `research/site-prior-art/T6.md`, lines 65-92, 94-110, and 120-122; preserve vendor-claim, inference, screenshot, and page-read-status distinctions.
- **file:** `outputs/sections/subsections/S7.3.md`
