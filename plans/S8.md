### S8.1 What transfers from game UX and what does not
- **question:** Which game-derived interaction ideas have evidence relevant to a farmer-facing map, and which claims in the supplied Wild Rift analogy overreach?
- **reader:** Designers familiar with touch games but not necessarily with HCI study design, agricultural workflows, or scientific uncertainty.
- **entry:** The supplied Game UX.md maps Wild Rift's HUD, map, pings, controls, radial actions, and alerts to farming, while T6 says product screenshots do not validate scientific performance and T1 documents substantial limits in satellite spatial and temporal support.
- **point:** Transfer bounded mechanics such as a stable spatial overview, reachable frequent controls, and optional detail only as hypotheses to test; game familiarity, urgency cues, and reward loops do not establish farmer usability, trust, or decision quality.
- **chain:** Separate observed game-interface features from measured user outcomes; summarize cognitive-load and split-attention evidence, including the 50-study meta-analysis and the 58-comparison meta-analysis; then pair it with the 146-student study that found no format effect and critiques of cognitive-load construct measurement; compare the evidence for thumb target size and reach with the small-screen, grip, and user differences that make a universal bottom-right thumb zone unjustified; end with a transfer table linking each mechanic to a farmer task, evidence, counter-evidence, and test.
- **principle:** Borrow a mechanism only when its task and user conditions resemble the source study, label the transfer as a design hypothesis, and measure success in the target farming context rather than assuming game engagement is decision support.
- **instance:** A persistent field overview and a discoverable anomaly-to-field navigation action may help orient a user; a combat alert should not label an uncertain satellite proxy as a confirmed threat, and a Wild Rift control placement must not be prescribed as a universal farmer thumb zone.
- **limit:** Education and menu-selection studies do not establish performance while walking, wearing gloves, sharing devices, working in sunlight or noise, or making consequential agronomic decisions; the supplied game UX notes are not a source of validation.
- **budget:** 640 words.
- **boundary:** This subsection owns the evidence-transfer method and game-pattern limits; S8.2 specifies concrete map controls, S8.5 owns uncertainty communication and escalation decisions.
- **source:** `Game UX.md`, lines 9-16 and 27-77; `research/site-prior-art/T6.md`, lines 94-110; `research/nasa-data/T1.md`, lines 9-27 and 171-182; verify the listed cognitive-load and thumb studies before final prose.

### S8.2 A map that carries the answer: thumb zones, radial actions, progressive disclosure
- **question:** How should a mobile map expose the field comparison and its next actions without obscuring spatial evidence or forcing every user into a game-like gesture?
- **reader:** A product designer shaping the map for small-screen use by farmers and agronomists with mixed device sizes, grips, and familiarity.
- **entry:** Game UX.md proposes a clear map center, thumb-side controls, radial actions, and progressively revealed layers; empirical studies measure pointing and menu selection under narrower lab conditions.
- **point:** Keep the selected field, comparison result, and map context spatially connected; make frequent actions large and reachable; offer visible labelled alternatives to gestures; treat radial menus and hidden detail as optional patterns whose speed, discoverability, and cognitive benefit depend on users and task.
- **chain:** Explain Fitts' law as a target-size and movement-distance model, then qualify it with studies finding orientation, grip, device size, and thumb biomechanics matter; compare radial and linear menus, noting pie-menu evidence mostly concerns small menus and radial layouts can reduce pointing distance while linear layouts can make visual search faster; address novice learning cost and gesture errors; compare progressive-disclosure claims with Springer and Whittaker's observed distraction from incremental transparency and a later 32-participant study where detail increased understanding and cognitive load while disclosure did not reduce load; derive concrete testable map rules, not a fixed wireframe.
- **principle:** Give actions an explicit label and reachable touch target, preserve a direct tap or button path, make a radial action menu small and contextual if user testing supports it, and keep the evidence and its location available while showing deeper detail on request.
- **instance:** From a selected field, a labelled action panel can expose “compare rotations,” “view evidence,” and “ask an agronomist”; an optional radial shortcut may open the same actions but cannot be the only route or hide uncertainty behind an icon.
- **limit:** Fitts' law does not alone predict one-thumb comfort; radial-menu speed findings do not automatically carry to a map under changing content; progressive disclosure can add retrieval steps, conceal useful evidence, or increase perceived learning without reducing measured load.
- **budget:** 700 words.
- **boundary:** This subsection owns map layout and control-choice hypotheses; S8.1 owns cross-domain transfer validity, S8.3 owns query semantics, and S8.5 owns the meaning of risk and uncertainty displays.
- **source:** `Game UX.md`, lines 37-64; verify full methods and limits in thumb studies at `https://doi.org/10.1145/2957265.2961864` and `https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36161546`, M3 at `https://doi.org/10.1145/3173574.3173823`, progressive disclosure at `https://arxiv.org/abs/1811.02164` and `https://doi.org/10.1145/3742413.3789087`; use external search/extraction as leads, not substitute citations.

### S8.3 Text-to-SQL over a geospatial schema without lying
- **question:** How can natural-language queries over fields, seasons, satellite products, and spatial observations avoid returning plausible but wrongly joined evidence?
- **reader:** A team building a text-to-SQL assistant that may know SQL syntax but has not designed the agricultural schema or semantic validation tests.
- **entry:** A query can execute successfully and still answer for the wrong field or season; production-schema research reports runnable wrong answers when join paths, reference values, or hidden schema conventions are wrong.
- **point:** Text-to-SQL is trustworthy only when the schema makes spatial and temporal meaning explicit, a deterministic layer constrains joins and operations, tests try to falsify semantic correctness, and the interface discloses the query's field, season, source, support, and unresolved ambiguity.
- **chain:** Define a proposed typed schema with `field(field_id, geom, jurisdiction)`, `field_season(season_id, field_id, start_date, end_date, crop_id, history_status)`, `grid_cell(cell_id, grid_id, geom)`, `field_cell(field_id, cell_id, grid_id, overlap_area_m2, weight)`, `product(product_id, variable, native_support_m, units, processing_version)`, and `observation(observation_id, product_id, cell_id, observed_at, value, qa_status, source_uri)`; state foreign keys, uniqueness, units, coordinate-reference system, null meaning, effective spatial support, and temporal-window semantics; show the plausible wrong join that uses `ST_Intersects(field.geom, cell.geom)` and counts every boundary-touching pixel as a whole field observation, thereby duplicating shared edge cells and making an adjacent field's stress look like this field's signal; replace that with a versioned overlap-weight membership table, quality-filtered aggregation, explicit season and product predicates, and area-weighted numerator/denominator; constrain the assistant to SELECT-only tools and an allowlisted schema with explicit join paths; validate parser/AST, column and join allowlists, row/area/time invariants, adversarial fixtures with adjacent fields and missing seasons, comparison against analyst-authored gold queries, and result-level semantic checks; disclose SQL, resolved field/season, data provenance, effective support, exclusions, missingness, and uncertainty to the user.
- **principle:** Execution success is necessary but not sufficient; database structure, invariant tests, and plain-language disclosure must prevent or reveal semantic errors, especially joins that are valid SQL but assign evidence to the wrong field or period.
- **instance:** A map question such as “Which of my last three seasons had the strongest moisture evidence?” must resolve one field identifier, an explicit season set, QA-valid observations at their actual support, and a declared aggregation before rendering results; an adjacent-field fixture must prove that a boundary cell cannot silently count as an unweighted whole-field observation.
- **limit:** The schema and validators are proposed, not built or evaluated here; a correct join cannot repair misdrawn boundaries, wrong crop labels, biased products, missing ground truth, or unsupported causal claims; model-generated explanations cannot substitute for deterministic verification.
- **budget:** 800 words.
- **boundary:** This subsection owns database semantics, join prevention, and SQL validation; S9.3 owns the planned service/tool architecture and runnable build boundaries, while S9.1 owns raster ingestion and grid alignment.
- **source:** `research/nasa-data/T1.md`, lines 9-27 and 171-196, for product support, quality and access limits; `research/site-prior-art/T6.md`, lines 53-63 for the gap between catalogue code and analysis; verify FLINT at `https://doi.org/10.48550/arxiv.2610.03524` and any additional current text-to-SQL evaluations before citing accuracy claims.
- **file:** `outputs/sections/subsections/S8.3.md`

### S8.4 Voice in a noisy field
- **question:** What role can speech safely play when a farmer's hands or attention are occupied and field noise can corrupt recognition?
- **reader:** A product and engineering team deciding whether voice should query data, navigate the map, or change persistent records.
- **entry:** The research premise is a field-use voice control; external speech studies identify ambient-noise, speaker-variation, and recognition-error constraints, while T6 documents account and data tasks that should not be triggered by a misunderstood phrase.
- **point:** Use a small, trained command grammar for reversible navigation and read-only queries, show or speak back the recognized command, and require explicit confirmation through a visible or spoken acknowledgement before any action writes or changes saved farm data.
- **chain:** Contrast constrained grammars, which can improve recognition when alternatives are discriminable, with free dictation's open vocabulary and noise burden; note reported industrial ASR difficulties above 81 dB and that a confidence-based error-detection study's apparent 85% result was not significantly different from its 80% baseline; derive command classes, confidence fallback, confirm/cancel behavior, and no-network/manual fallback; explicitly prohibit executing a write on a low-confidence parse or inferred place/field name.
- **principle:** Limit the language surface, make recognition errors recoverable, keep read-only lookup separate from state-changing actions, and require a second explicit confirmation for every write regardless of the recognizer's confidence.
- **instance:** Small commands such as “show field,” “compare rotations,” “next season,” “zoom to field,” “repeat,” and “cancel” can be mapped to labelled controls; “save this boundary,” “change crop history,” or “share my farm” must present the exact proposed change and await confirmation.
- **limit:** Noise thresholds and error rates are device-, speaker-, microphone-, language-, and context-specific; the cited industrial and assistive studies do not validate performance for Brazilian Portuguese, US farm accents, gloves, tractors, or this app, and no voice implementation has been run.
- **budget:** 600 words.
- **boundary:** This subsection owns speech input, recognition feedback, and write confirmation; S8.3 owns query interpretation and database safety, while S8.5 owns when the scientific recommendation itself must be withheld or escalated.
- **source:** `Game UX.md`, lines 37-64, as a design hypothesis only; verify `https://arxiv.org/abs/1811.06096`, `https://doi.org/10.3390/mti8020008`, and `https://doi.org/10.1145/3733155.3734896` before final prose and preserve their distinct study contexts.

### S8.5 Abstention: showing uncertainty, and when to ask your agronomist
- **question:** How should the interface communicate evidence gaps and uncertainty, and when should it stop short of a recommendation?
- **reader:** A farmer or agronomist reading a comparison that combines satellite proxies, local records, and model assumptions.
- **entry:** T1 shows that many products are spatially coarse, cloud-limited, model-derived, delayed, or unable to establish yield or causal rotation effects; uncertainty displays themselves can be misunderstood.
- **point:** Separate observed evidence, model estimates, missing evidence, and farmer-entered facts; attach uncertainty to the decision and its action boundary; abstain or ask an agronomist when data validity, spatial support, crop context, or expert judgment is required rather than flattening uncertainty into a confident color or score.
- **chain:** Distinguish retrieval/coverage gaps, measurement error, model uncertainty, and disagreement in local assumptions; describe deterministic-construal evidence, including that visually displayed probability ranges can be read as deterministic values; avoid invented confidence percentages and uncalibrated stop thresholds; design a visible evidence card with source, acquisition date, effective support, QA, missing seasons, and a textual uncertainty statement; define planned escalation triggers such as conflicting sources, insufficient valid seasons, unsupported crop/region, or a decision requiring field inspection; require farmer correction or expert review and preserve the system's abstention reason.
- **principle:** Make uncertainty legible in words and provenance, tie it to a specific decision and next action, and abstain where the evidence cannot justify the inference; color and a single scalar score must never stand alone.
- **instance:** A SMAP 9 km posting represents a roughly 36 km radiometer footprint, an ECOSTRESS clear-sky overpass is irregular rather than continuous, and POWER is gridded reanalysis; these can support context or scouting but not a fabricated field-level causal claim about yield or soil health.
- **limit:** The cited uncertainty-communication work concerns forecast judgments, not validated agricultural abstention thresholds; no probability calibration, agronomist protocol, crop-specific boundary, or escalation threshold is established by the packs.
- **budget:** 660 words.
- **boundary:** This subsection owns confidence, evidence-gap disclosure, abstention, and expert escalation; S8.1 owns general game-pattern transfer, S8.2 owns map control layout, and S9.2 owns the planned score's data and risk decomposition.
- **source:** `research/nasa-data/T1.md`, lines 9-27 and 171-196; `research/site-prior-art/T6.md`, lines 41-49 and 108-122; verify Joslyn & Savelli at `https://doi.org/10.3389/fcomp.2020.590232` before stating visual-uncertainty effects.

## Open questions and risks
- The supplied Wild Rift UX file is an unverified research aid: its references and specific claims about layout, cognitive load, and user benefits need independent source verification, and should not be cited as evidence.
- Study populations and tasks differ from farm work; do not claim that classroom cognitive-load evidence or lab menu-speed results prove field performance.
- Counter-evidence matters: split-attention effects have not replicated uniformly; thumb reach depends on grip and device; radial menus can impair visual search and novice use; progressive disclosure may not reduce cognitive load; detailed explanations can improve understanding while raising effort.
- The schema must make spatial support, source version, season, QA, geometry overlap, units, and missing values explicit; a passing SQL execution is not a semantic correctness test.
- No agricultural user testing, voice evaluation, uncertainty calibration, field observation, or expert escalation policy has been performed for this app; those remain planned tests and decisions.
- Verify the current full text and methods of all external studies before final prose; search snippets and abstracts are discovery leads, not settled evidence.
