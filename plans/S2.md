### S2.1 SMAP soil moisture: a 36 km footprint reading the top 5 cm
- **question:** What does SMAP soil-moisture data physically represent, and at what scale can it inform a rotation decision?
- **reader:** App builder or agronomy reader who recognizes soil moisture but may interpret each 9 km grid cell as an independent farm-scale measurement.
- **entry:** A daily 9 km-posted SMAP Enhanced L3 surface-soil-moisture map and the tempting inference that adjacent cells or one farm have separate observed values.
- **point:** SMAP radiometer retrievals respond to near-surface moisture in roughly the top 5 cm, but the Enhanced 9 km grid is interpolated from an approximately 36 km native footprint; posting resolution is not independent information or field-level support.
- **chain:** The L-band radiometer measures emitted microwave brightness temperature; retrieval converts the signal to surface moisture through a vegetation/soil-temperature model; the surface support is about 0–5 cm; the 9 km EASE-Grid posting is Backus-Gilbert interpolation over roughly a 33 km contributing domain from a ~36 km footprint; daily compositing and 2–3 day global revisit do not create finer spatial information; frozen ground, snow, water, steep terrain, urban fractions, dense vegetation and residual RFI impair retrieval.
- **principle:** A finer posted grid cannot exceed the information support of the instrument footprint and retrieval assumptions; use it as broad surface-water context, not as a probe in every pixel.
- **instance:** A block-scale indication of surface drying or recharge between rainfall events may inform whether a planting window merits local checking; the T1 capability pack frames useful inference around roughly 10–36 km and expressly excludes farm-gate scheduling and per-field claims.
- **limit:** SMAP surface soil moisture does not directly measure the crop root zone, irrigation applied, or moisture in a particular field. Do not confuse L2 half-orbit timing with daily L3 composites, or interpret 9 km posting as the 36 km footprint becoming multiple independent observations. State version and date for the 2025-01-01 to 2026-11-10 current facts; no granules were downloaded in this research.
- **budget:** 650 words.
- **boundary:** This subsection owns observed SMAP radiometer surface retrievals and scale; S2.5 owns GRACE's gravity-derived total-water-storage trend and the contrast with modeled states.
- **source:** `/research/nasa-data/T1.md` capability table lines 9–13; SPL3SMP_E lines 30–42; SPL2SMP_E lines 44–56; Cannot do lines 171–180; source dates in lines 215–221.

### S2.2 ECOSTRESS evapotranspiration, stress index and land surface temperature
- **question:** What can irregular ECOSTRESS ET, evaporative-stress and temperature products contribute, and what do they not establish?
- **reader:** Product team or farmer-facing writer accustomed to treating a map of evapotranspiration, stress, or temperature as a continuous field condition.
- **entry:** A 70 m ECOSTRESS map that looks much more field-detailed than SMAP and appears to show water use or heat stress.
- **point:** ECOSTRESS provides useful clear-sky, overpass-specific scouting signals, but ET and ESI are derived model estimates and LST is radiometric surface skin temperature; irregular acquisitions, clouds and input/model limitations prevent them from establishing continuous seasonal water use, root-zone status, or rotation-caused outcomes.
- **chain:** ECOSTRESS measures thermal-infrared radiance; TES retrieves LST/emissivity with an ill-posed inversion and cloud/emissivity risks; JET combines LST with GEOS-5 meteorology, downscaled soil moisture and HLS inputs across ET models, so ET is modeled flux rather than direct water-use measurement; ESI is an actual/potential ET ratio without a stated standardized multi-week climatological window; tiled outputs are 70 m but acquisitions are irregular, with mission-average revisit described as 1–5 days rather than fixed; on 2026-11-10 latest JET/ESI granules were dated 2026-10-28 while LSTE was dated 2026-11-07.
- **principle:** Fine output cells and vivid thermal contrast do not imply a continuous observation, a direct crop response, or causal evidence; interpret each product according to its measured input and derived quantity.
- **instance:** A sufficiently large field or subfield showing an anomalous clear-overpass ET/temperature pattern can be flagged for scouting or a local irrigation investigation; ESI may serve as a same-day flag, with explicit caveat that high-stress performance concerns were reported and the cited validation is not direct validation of this tiled V003 dataset.
- **limit:** No seasonal ET total, continuous daily field series, crop yield, water savings, or rotation superiority claim follows from one irregular pass. Clouds and gaps can remove precisely the dates a decision needs; surface skin temperature is not air or root-zone temperature. Describe latency as product-specific: T1 gives nominal FAQ values but also the observed 2026-11-10 CMR lags and says the JET ATBD supplies no numeric latency.
- **budget:** 700 words.
- **boundary:** This subsection owns thermal measurements and ECOSTRESS-derived flux/stress products; S2.3 owns precipitation estimates and IMERG's version/latency issue, while S2.4 owns optical reflectance and vegetation-index interpretation.
- **source:** `/research/nasa-data/T1.md` table lines 15–17; ECO_L3T_JET, ECO_L4T_ESI, ECO_L2T_LSTE entries lines 87–106; Cannot do lines 177, 186–187; source dates lines 237–253.

### S2.3 GPM IMERG rainfall, and the halt in the Final product
- **question:** Which IMERG product can support rainfall context, at what scale and freshness, and what does the V07 Final halt mean for current use?
- **reader:** App builder or grower who expects satellite precipitation to provide a current rain gauge at field scale.
- **entry:** A daily precipitation grid, with the assumption that one readily named product is both current and gauge-corrected.
- **point:** IMERG is a gridded satellite/microwave-infrared precipitation estimate, not a farm rain gauge; V07 Final supports retrospective regional totals only through 2025-09-30, while Late offers faster but not Final gauge-adjusted context and carries a documented latency discrepancy.
- **chain:** Constellation microwave estimates are motion-vector interpolated and filled with infrared estimates between overpasses; Final adds later monthly GPCC gauge adjustment; the grid is 0.1 degrees, approximately 11 km at the equator, and daily sums aggregate half-hourly estimates; V07 Final halted after 2025-09-30 during the V08 transition; as of source checks 2026-11-10 the planned V08 Final fall-2026 date was only projected; Late is available next day but official nominal latency conflicts, 12 hours in the ATBD versus 14 hours on the product page, and is not Final gauge-calibrated.
- **principle:** A higher-quality retrospective product may be stale, while a near-real-time product trades the later gauge adjustment for timeliness; neither grid is field precipitation truth.
- **instance:** Use completed-season Final data through the stated V07 cutoff for regional rainfall context, or Late as a next-day regional planting-window clue checked against a local gauge or farmer report. Keep source version and period visible.
- **limit:** Do not imply V07 Final contains post-2025-09-30 values, assert that V08 Final actually launched absent a current verification, or use the ~11 km grid as an irrigation trigger for a field. Skill weakens over frozen surfaces, complex terrain, coasts and warm-rain regimes; no actual field rain-gauge validation for a target farm is present in T1.
- **budget:** 500 words.
- **boundary:** This subsection owns precipitation and the Final/Late latency-quality tradeoff; S2.2 owns ET/stress/temperature, and S2.5 owns broad multi-year storage, terrain, biomass and climate context.
- **source:** `/research/nasa-data/T1.md` table lines 18–19; GPM entries lines 108–120; Cannot do line 178; What I could not find lines 188–189; source dates lines 254–263.

### S2.4 HLS, Landsat and MODIS optical: revisit, cloud, and what a vegetation index omits
- **question:** How can HLS and MODIS observations show crop cover or phenology, and why do revisit and vegetation indices not reveal full crop history or performance?
- **reader:** Map or agent designer who treats a vegetation-colored layer as a crop label, a stress measure, or a yield estimate.
- **entry:** 30 m harmonized Landsat/Sentinel imagery and MODIS greenness composites with an apparent time series over a field.
- **point:** HLS can support locally checked observation of seasonal cover and possible crop-sequence patterns, while MODIS adds coarse regional phenology; clouds, composites, mixed pixels and index physics mean neither product alone identifies a crop, proves management, or measures rotation outcomes.
- **chain:** HLS harmonizes Landsat OLI and Sentinel-2 MSI surface reflectance to a 30 m grid; combined nominal cycle is 8 days for Landsat 8/9 and 5 days for two Sentinel-2 satellites, but clouds, processing/QA and scene failures interrupt usable observations; output resolution is not the native resolution of every MSI band; reflectance and NDVI/EVI respond to canopy, pigment, soil and residue mixture, not crop identity or yield; MOD13Q1 selects a best-quality observation in each 16-day composite at 250 m, where NDVI can saturate at high leaf area and short events can disappear; MOD11A1 gives daily 1 km radiometric skin temperature but is not a field thermometer; CMR acquisition/ingest dates are examples, not product-specific guarantees.
- **principle:** Optical time series show what the sensor sees under usable conditions; classification and agronomic meaning require clear-scene coverage, quality control, local validation and non-satellite history.
- **instance:** Compare clear HLS observations across seasons for candidate cover/fallow or double-crop patterns, preserve QA and collection/vintage, then ask the user to confirm crop identity and dates; use MODIS only for broader phenology context rather than small-field compliance.
- **limit:** No unvalidated classifier claim that HLS identifies a cover crop, crop species or rotation; no yield, soil-health, or belowground inference. Do not treat 30 m as ground truth, convert nominal revisit into guaranteed clear observations, compare historical HLS without known-issues context, or claim MODIS resolves small fields. Terra was scheduled to end operations in 2027-02 per T1 status checked 2026-11-10; present this as a dated scheduled event, not a completed event.
- **budget:** 750 words.
- **boundary:** This subsection owns reflected optical signals, vegetation indices and optical revisit/cloud limitations; S2.2 owns ECOSTRESS thermal products and S2.3 owns rainfall.
- **source:** `/research/nasa-data/T1.md` table lines 20–23; HLSL30/HLSS30 and MODIS entries lines 122–148; Cannot do lines 174, 179–180; source dates lines 264–283.

### S2.5 GRACE, SRTM, GEDI and POWER: trend, terrain, biomass and climate envelope
- **question:** What different contextual roles can GRACE, SRTM, GEDI and POWER play, and how must the report distinguish observations from model states and gridded context?
- **reader:** Decision-support team combining diverse datasets into one map who may mistakenly treat all layers as equivalent observations at their pixel size.
- **entry:** A proposed composite map mixing GRACE water storage, static SRTM terrain, GEDI biomass and daily POWER climate values.
- **point:** These products answer distinct contextual questions at distinct supports: GRACE senses broad monthly total mass change, SRTM records static 2000 radar-derived elevation, GEDI provides sampled lidar inputs to a modeled biomass grid ending in 2023, and POWER supplies gridded model/reanalysis climate; none is an observed field-scale root-zone water measurement, and SMAP L4 root zone is a model state rather than an observation.
- **chain:** GRACE/GRACE-FO infers mass change from satellite-to-satellite gravity/range measurements; native mascons are about 300–330 km while 0.5-degree cells are oversampled posting, and anomalies combine water components relative to a 2004.0–2009.999 baseline; SRTM is a single 2000 C-band radar acquisition with 30 m posting, canopy phase-center and no repeat record; GEDI L4B extrapolates sparse lidar observations through a model to 1 km biomass estimates from 2019-04-18 to 2023-03-16; POWER combines reanalysis/model meteorology and satellite-related solar inputs on about 0.5-degree daily grids, with documentation conflict about hydrology grid dimensions; separately, SMAP L4's 0–100 cm root-zone value is Catchment land-surface-model output constrained by brightness-temperature assimilation, not a direct observation, and remains model-only during SMAP outages.
- **principle:** Keep measurement, model state, model-derived estimate, static historical measurement and grid posting visibly distinct; spatially aligning layers cannot make their physical support or uncertainty equal.
- **instance:** Use GRACE only for multi-year basin/aquifer-scale storage context with leakage and total-water-component caveats; SRTM for terrain screening, not current drainage; GEDI for historical forest/clearing safeguards, not annual crop biomass; POWER for long-term climate envelope, not farm water balance; if presenting SMAP L4 root-zone context, label it modeled 0–100 cm state with precipitation/model error and outage caveats.
- **limit:** GRACE cannot resolve a municipality or farm, or isolate groundwater without assumptions and other-component models; its 0.5-degree grid is not native resolution. SRTM cannot observe post-2000 terrain modification. GEDI is not wall-to-wall and its forest-trained biomass model does not transfer automatically to annual crops. POWER is not a field sensor. SMAP L4 root zone must never be described as measured; a gap-free output during SMAP outages is not evidence of concomitant satellite observation. Do not infer a rotation caused basin water change, soil carbon, biomass gain, or yield.
- **budget:** 650 words.
- **boundary:** This subsection owns long-term water-storage, terrain, biomass and climate-envelope context, plus the critical modeled-root-zone contrast; S2.1 owns SMAP observed surface-moisture retrieval details, and S3 owns non-NASA soil/crop/management sources.
- **source:** `/research/nasa-data/T1.md` table lines 13–14, 24–26; GRACE entry lines 72–84; SRTM lines 150–155; GEDI lines 157–162; POWER lines 164–169; Cannot do lines 173, 176, 181–182; What I could not find lines 191–196.
