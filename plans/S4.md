## Entries
### S4.1 Cash rent, tenure and the landlord veto
- **question:** Which parts of a US rotation can a tenant decide alone, and which require a landowner's consent or a longer lease?
- **reader:** App designers and US row-crop readers who understand rent in general but may assume operators control every field practice.
- **entry:** A farmer weighs a longer-term soil, forage or third-crop sequence against annual rent, renewal risk and the written lease.
- **point:** Tenure and lease terms set the feasible time horizon; they do not prove that a landlord chooses the crop or that a higher rent makes corn the right answer.
- **chain:** Fixed cash rent remains due when yields or prices fall; annual terms can leave an operator unable to recover multi-year rotation investments; crop-share arrangements distribute some output and cost risk; written restrictions, compensation or a longer term can change who has authority. The 2026 central Illinois example's projected −$15/ac cash-rent return versus +$18/ac under a modeled 50/50 crop share is a local budget, not an observed national outcome. Satellite imagery can identify crop history and cover patterns, but it cannot reveal lease clauses, rent, renewal promises or consent.
- **principle:** Treat lease horizon, rent, crop restrictions and landowner permission as explicit user-provided constraints; do not infer them from crop maps or regional rental shares.
- **instance:** Use the Illinois 2026 budget for the rent-risk mechanism and the Practical Farmers of Iowa case in which Paul Thompson and Elena Meyer chose tenant Josh Nelson for no-till, covers and a third crop. Attribute the conditional rent-discount example precisely: up to 30 small-grain acres were offered a significant discount, while the correction says the flex lease itself did not include that discount. Do not turn the case into a general landlord veto. (T3, “Lease duration, landlord terms, and decision authority,” lines 49–55.)
- **limit:** The pack finds no representative estimate of lease duration or how often landlords block rotations; a landowner may be non-operating, and a lease can leave substantial operator freedom. 39% of all US farmland rented is not the same as more than half of cropland rented. The app can display satellite-observed history but only the tenant/owner or contract can settle these terms.
- **budget:** 660 words.
- **boundary:** This subsection owns control rights, time horizon and rent exposure. S4.2 owns federal program and insurance design; S4.3 owns market netback and crop-specific margin; do not repeat their calculations here.
- **source:** T3, Driver table lines 9–11; “Cash rent and land cost” lines 33–39; “Tenure mix and fixed-rent risk” lines 41–47; “Lease duration, landlord terms, and decision authority” lines 49–55; “Tenant and landlord split” lines 219–229.

### S4.2 Crop insurance, base acres and programme payments
- **question:** Which public payments and risk protections actually constrain a crop choice, and which should not be mistaken for a planted-acre mandate?
- **reader:** US growers, lenders and app builders familiar with farm programmes but needing the difference between insurance, program history and temporary aid made explicit.
- **entry:** A user compares two crop sequences while carrying a field's crop-specific APH, insurance price and coverage, FSA base history, lease share and any crop-year payment.
- **point:** A decision-support tool must separate the crop-specific insured floor from historically decoupled base payments and one-time planted-acre aid; neither base acres nor a headline rate alone selects a crop.
- **chain:** For 2026 the projected prices were $4.62/bu corn and $11.09/bu soy, but these are price inputs rather than a farm's guarantee; RMA APH and coverage are crop-specific. Legacy ARC/PLC base generally did not dictate current planting, although the one-time 2026 additional-base allocation used 2019–23 history. FBA was tied to 2025 planted acres and is not recurring margin. Lease terms determine who receives or shares income; lenders and insurance agents may clarify coverage. Satellite data can supply crop history or conditions, not private APH, insurance-unit terms, FSA records, or payment entitlement.
- **principle:** Present insurance, programme payments and farm-specific records as distinct line items with dates and eligibility; require the farmer, lender or agent to confirm actual coverage and payment status.
- **instance:** Use the T3 figures: $4.62/bu and $11.09/bu 2026 projected prices; the 30-million-acre national base expansion was prorated by 3.69%; and 2025 FBA rates were $44.36/ac corn and $30.88/ac soybeans. Explain what those figures do and do not establish, not a hypothetical farmer's payment.
- **limit:** Public data cannot match farm-level insured acres/APH, FSA base, owner/operator shares and current planting. A payment rate is not a guaranteed farm payment, and a national base allocation does not show whether a specific farm changes its rotation. Distinguish program status and dates; do not imply 2026 payments based on future or unverified eligibility.
- **budget:** 660 words.
- **boundary:** This subsection owns insurance and government programme mechanisms. S4.1 owns lease authority, and S4.3 owns expected market revenue and local basis. Avoid treating an insurance guarantee as crop profitability.
- **source:** T3, “Base acres and commodity-program payments” lines 12, 57–63; “One-time planted-acre bridge aid” lines 13, 65–71; “Crop insurance guarantee and own-crop history” lines 14, 73–79; “Tenant and landlord split” lines 219–229.
- **file:** `outputs/sections/subsections/S4-2-insurance-base-acres-payments.md`

### S4.3 Price, basis and where a rotation stops paying
- **question:** How does a US farmer compare the expected return of a crop sequence after field-specific prices, basis, input costs and harvest logistics?
- **reader:** App builders and farm decision-makers who know futures prices but need a farmgate, sequence-level comparison rather than a single ratio.
- **entry:** A user sees a national crop signal and asks whether a local buyer, yield history, rotation benefit or sunk input bill changes the field's net return.
- **point:** Crop choice must be compared as a local multi-year netback, not read directly from futures, a single crop price ratio or a headline cost.
- **chain:** Start with field yields and crop-specific variable costs; add local elevator/processor basis, delivery timing, drying, haul and storage; account for pre-applied nitrogen, rotational yield effects and existing equipment. The dated 2026-11-04 DTN snapshot showed corn Decatur +10¢ Z versus Fort Dodge −40¢ Z and soybean Decatur +6¢ F versus Grand Island −100¢ F, demonstrating location and delivery basis, not a permanent ranking. Ryan Facemire of Franklin, Indiana, said his late-March 2026 bids were $4.52/bu corn and $11/bu soy and that marginal corn needed 190 bu/ac to break even versus 40 bu/ac soy; he switched all acres to soy. Attribute this as his operation-specific account, not a national threshold. Satellite crop/yield history may inform observed field conditions but cannot supply forward bids, invoices, contracts, input commitments or an elevator settlement.
- **principle:** Show assumptions, delivery point, date, units, sunk versus avoidable costs and rotation horizon; request the operator's current bids and costs before presenting a margin comparison.
- **instance:** Build around the dated basis spread and Facemire's attributed case, then note the 2016 field-record analysis estimated 4.3% rainfed continuous-corn and 10.3% continuous-soy yield penalties, with a 1.7% irrigated continuous-corn estimate. Do not collapse different dates, locations or measures into a single crop score.
- **limit:** A one-day basis snapshot is not a forward bid; local haul, contract, quality and timing can reverse it. Historical field averages are not a specific field forecast. Facemire's decision is a single farmer case; the pack has no national causal elasticity for price-to-acreage response.
- **budget:** 660 words.
- **boundary:** This subsection owns market netback, input exposure, rotational yield and crop logistics. S4.1 owns tenure constraints; S4.2 owns insurance/programme terms; S4.4 owns binding water allocation and S4.5 owns pest/adviser decisions.
- **source:** T3, “Futures price and corn/soy price ratio” lines 15, 81–87; “Local elevator / processor basis and delivery” lines 16, 89–95; “Nitrogen/fertilizer cost and sunk preplant inputs” lines 17, 97–103; “Equipment width” lines 19, 113–119; “Harvest labor” lines 20, 121–127; “Rotational yield” lines 21, 129–135; “Local crop market” lines 29, 193–199.
- **file:** `outputs/sections/subsections/S4-3-price-basis-rotation-margin.md`

### S4.4 Water: the Ogallala, Kansas LEMAs and SGMA fallowing
- **question:** When does water availability change the feasible rotation, and what can satellite observations establish versus what a water right or district must confirm?
- **reader:** US farmers, water managers and app designers who need to compare western Kansas irrigation limits with California groundwater and surface-water constraints.
- **entry:** An irrigator compares crops, pumping and fallowing under a particular field's water right, meter readings, allocation, district delivery and crop water need.
- **point:** Water stress and remotely sensed crop conditions flag risk, but an allocation or pumping rule is a field- and jurisdiction-specific constraint that satellites cannot settle.
- **chain:** Kansas High Plains aquifer levels had a provisional 0.2-ft overall rise in 2025 after five years of decline, while the 1996–2025 mean was −0.57 ft/year and GMD3 fell 0.62 ft in 2025. Sheridan County 6's five-year LEMA allocation and later estimates show possible crop-mix and water-intensity responses, but a cap binds only when it is below desired use; outcomes differ by study and weather. In California, SGMA pumping plans, district surface-water allocations and trading interact with perennial orchard investment; PPIC's 500,000–900,000-acre fallowing number is a 2040 scenario, not observed SGMA-caused fallow. Remote sensing can show crop, vegetation and surface conditions at its resolution; it cannot identify a parcel's legal right, meter use, GSA fee, water delivery promise or farmer's intended fallow reason.
- **principle:** Link satellite evidence to a dated, uncertainty-labeled water-risk layer and ask the operator or water authority for actual rights, allocations, pumping and delivery; never infer legal entitlement from imagery.
- **instance:** Contrast western Kansas LEMA evidence (including the 55 acre-inch/acre five-year SD-6 allocation) with the California SGMA scenario and 2026 overdraft update. Label measured aquifer observation, modeled fallowing scenario and proposed or enacted local rule separately.
- **limit:** A regional aquifer level is not well-level supply; rainfall affected the 2025 Kansas increase. Kansas findings cannot be exported to every Ogallala state. PPIC projections are not measured fallowing attributable to SGMA; orchards cannot be treated like annual grain fields. User and water-district records are required to resolve rights and actual deliveries.
- **budget:** 660 words.
- **boundary:** This subsection owns water availability, allocation and fallowing. S4.3 owns crop economics; S4.1 owns land tenure. S6.3 owns dated climate and remote-sensing product availability, not the local legal water decision.
- **source:** T3, “Ogallala availability and Kansas LEMA allocation” lines 25, 161–167; “California SGMA, surface-water reliability and fallowing” lines 26, 169–175; “Region notes” lines 203–217; T5, “US growing-season drought footprint” and “Greater GMD 4 LEMA renewal” lines 17, 19, 51–56, 67–72.

### S4.5 Resistance, frost risk and the agronomist channel
- **question:** How should a rotation recommendation account for local weed resistance, frost and the adviser who translates field conditions into an actionable crop plan?
- **reader:** US operators, agronomists and app teams who may use satellite signals but need field diagnosis and human advice for biological risk.
- **entry:** A grower weighs a crop or herbicide sequence after weed escapes, stage-specific freeze risk, field history and a conversation with an agronomist or retailer.
- **point:** A satellite-assisted recommendation can support scouting and timing, but resistance assays, crop-stage diagnosis and an adviser or grower decision are needed before changing a field plan.
- **chain:** The pack reports four suspected glufosinate-resistant waterhemp populations in an Illinois study, with 20% survival at one field site's labeled rate; resistance and weed seedbank are field-specific. Frost injury depends on temperature duration, wheat stage, variety, moisture and field position; K-State advises 10–14 warm days before assessment after the March 2026 freeze. Advisers influence scouting, inputs, timing and custom service, though Purdue's 2025 survey ranked retailer/dealer resources below ag media and peers as a new-idea source. Mike Brelsford, a 6,000-acre Iowa farmer, said a second combine would require eight workers and hiring eight at harvest was “virtually impossible”; attribute the quote and mark it as one farm account. Satellites may flag crop/cover anomalies, not diagnose herbicide resistance, determine wheat injury or verify adviser availability.
- **principle:** Make satellite observations triggers for field scouting and human review, not diagnoses or automatic prescriptions; record who confirms an agronomic constraint and what evidence was used.
- **instance:** Use the Illinois waterhemp research, K-State's 2026 freeze assessment, Purdue survey shares, and Brelsford's explicitly attributed harvest-labor quote to demonstrate distinct scales of evidence. Avoid presenting sponsored retailer accounts as independent efficacy trials.
- **limit:** A tested population is not a regional resistance map; regional temperature is not a yield loss; a survey of large commercial producers is not all farmers. Individual farmer quotations illustrate experience rather than establish prevalence. A map cannot confirm lease, custom applicator, retailer or labor availability.
- **budget:** 660 words.
- **boundary:** This subsection owns biological risk, crop-stage frost diagnosis, adviser and operational confirmation. S4.3 owns price and net return; S4.4 owns water. Do not expand into S6's 2026-wide policy and climate chronology.
- **source:** T3, “Herbicide resistance and weed seedbank” lines 22, 137–143; “Agronomist / retailer recommendation and service” lines 24, 153–159; “Frost, freeze and growing-season window” lines 28, 185–191; “Harvest labor” lines 20, 121–127; T5, “Palmer amaranth spread and resistance” lines 40, 212–217.
