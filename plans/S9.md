### S9.1 Ingestion and the common grid
- **question:** What reproducible data path can bring NASA inputs to a comparable field view without pretending all products have the same native support or access conditions?
- **reader:** A developer preparing a small, judge-runnable field example who needs to separate discovery, download, raster processing, and scientific interpretation.
- **entry:** T6 reports that starter snippets ran at catalogue/API level, but did not download or inspect Earthdata science files or implement resampling; T1 records product-specific resolution, cadence, latency, quality limitations, and access.
- **point:** Implement ingestion as versioned, auditable stages and use a common display/analysis grid only as an alignment mechanism; preserve native support, product meaning, acquisition time, QA, and missingness alongside every output.
- **chain:** Pin the Python environment and `earthaccess==0.19.0`; search public CMR metadata without login, then require Earthdata Login for download; record collection/version, granule, geometry, dates, units, QA bands, and provenance in a manifest; set extent and CRS per study area rather than inventing one national projection; use a `rasterio==1.4.4` build, whose bundled GDAL library is 3.10.3 in the tested environment, and `rasterio.warp.reproject` with explicit source/destination CRS, transforms, dimensions, nodata and resampling; if using the external `gdalwarp` binary instead, require a GDAL 3.x CLI build and specify `-t_srs`, `-tr`, `-tap`, `-r`, `-srcnodata`, and `-dstnodata`; use nearest-neighbor for categorical QA and an appropriate continuous method for continuous variables; keep coarse and modeled layers at their true support and mask invalid cells rather than interpolating them into apparent detail; run geometry, CRS, bounds, nodata, QA, and value-range checks before rendering.
- **principle:** A common grid makes arrays align; it does not make the source observations equally precise, independent, timely, or field-resolving.
- **instance:** T1 says HLS output is 30 m, SMAP's 9 km posting derives from a roughly 36 km radiometer footprint, GRACE's 0.5 degree grid represents roughly 300-330 km mascons, and POWER is a coarse reanalysis grid; each must retain those support notes in the layer manifest and map legend.
- **limit:** No Earthdata granule was downloaded or inspected in T1/T6 because credentials were unavailable; no field polygon, projection choice, product subset, resampling output, or common grid has been selected or tested for this app.
- **budget:** 600 words.
- **boundary:** This subsection owns source discovery, access, raster alignment, and data QA; S9.2 owns score interpretation and rotations, and S9.4 owns the judge's environment and failure path.
- **source:** `research/nasa-data/T1.md`, lines 9-27, 30-70, 87-169, and 171-196; `research/site-prior-art/T6.md`, lines 51-63 and 114-119; command behavior references above must be matched to the actual GDAL and earthaccess builds when the implementation is chosen.

### S9.2 Scoring three rotations with evidence, gaps and risk
- **question:** How can a prototype compare a current rotation and alternatives without presenting a proxy score as a validated agronomic recommendation?
- **reader:** A product team and judge evaluating whether a comparison is traceable and useful, not whether an unvalidated model has established causation.
- **entry:** T6's minimum-useful prototype is a comparison of at least three rotations including the current one with evidence and risks; T1 identifies which observations support limited context versus which decisions they cannot establish.
- **point:** Present a transparent, multi-criteria scenario comparison whose evidence coverage and unresolved risks are visible beside each rotation; any numerical score is an explicit, versioned prioritization aid, not a prediction of yield, profit, or soil-health causality.
- **chain:** Define the field, crop sequence, season boundaries, current rotation, candidate rotations, jurisdiction, and farmer-supplied constraints before calculation; compute comparable per-season indicators only from QA-valid observations with known source, units, dates, native support, and coverage; keep observed, modeled, entered, and missing values distinct; show per-criterion contribution, evidence coverage, and risk flags; if the product exposes a composite score, normalize criteria with documented direction and units, expose the farmer-selected weights and score version, and provide sensitivity comparison when weights or evidence coverage change; identify unsupported or low-coverage dimensions as unavailable rather than silently filling them with a favorable default; do not present a winner if evidence or local agronomic constraints are insufficient.
- **principle:** A decision-support score should be decomposable into traceable evidence and declared preferences; missingness and uncertainty change how strongly an option can be compared, not the value that the system is entitled to invent.
- **instance:** Compare the current rotation with two alternatives; report HLS crop/cover evidence only with local classifier validation, use POWER as climate context, treat SMAP as coarse surface-moisture context and SMAP L4 root-zone moisture as modeled, and use ECOSTRESS only as an irregular clear-overpass scouting signal; disclose that none establishes rotation-caused yield or soil-health outcomes.
- **limit:** The packs specify neither crop-specific ground truth nor validated weights, risk thresholds, local calibration, economic model, or agronomist-approved decision rule; all proposed scoring and comparison behavior is planned and must be evaluated before recommendation language is used.
- **budget:** 600 words.
- **boundary:** This subsection owns scenario inputs, evidence accounting, and score/risk interpretation; S9.1 owns ingestion and support metadata, and S8.5 owns user-facing uncertainty and the agronomist escalation principle.
- **source:** `research/site-prior-art/T6.md`, lines 35-49, 41-48, and 57-63; `research/nasa-data/T1.md`, lines 11-26, 58-70, 87-169, and 171-196; do not infer agronomic validity from a successful query or a vendor score.

### S9.3 Agent boundary and validator
- **question:** What bounded agent architecture could answer questions over the proposed data while keeping spatial joins, writes, and unsupported claims under control?
- **reader:** An engineer implementing a natural-language assistant who needs exact boundaries between model behavior, deterministic data services, and user-approved actions.
- **entry:** S8.3 establishes that a syntactically valid query can join the wrong field or season; the source packs do not document an existing Field Shift agent, database, or guardrail implementation.
- **point:** Treat the language model as a request parser and query planner over an allowlisted, documented schema; execute validated, read-only query templates through deterministic tools; show the resolved field, season, join path, sources, and limitations; keep all state-changing tools outside the autonomous query path and require explicit user confirmation.
- **chain:** Normalize natural-language intent into a typed request with field ID, season interval, indicator, and aggregation; resolve each identifier against visible user-selectable records; retrieve only schema tables and foreign-key paths allowed for that question; validate SQL syntax, SELECT-only operation, identifiers, join constraints, spatial predicates, temporal bounds, row counts, and output units before execution; compare tests with analyst-authored gold queries and fixtures containing adjacent fields, shared boundaries, missing seasons, duplicate granules, and invalid QA; return data plus a provenance/evidence object, not a free-form factual answer alone; decline ambiguous IDs, unsupported crop questions, and write requests, then offer a correction or human-review path.
- **principle:** Make risky semantics explicit and deterministic at the data boundary; do not rely on prompt wording, model confidence, SQL execution success, or a generated explanation as the only guardrail.
- **instance:** A planned query tool might accept `field_id`, a bounded season list, an allowlisted metric, and an aggregation enum; it would return values, units, source granules, effective support, QA exclusions, overlap policy, and missingness; there is no such tool or schema in the repository yet.
- **limit:** Proposed schema, query validator, tests, agent model, storage, and tool calls are unbuilt; no model has been benchmarked for query accuracy or prompt-injection resistance, and no claim of safe deployment is verified.
- **budget:** 600 words.
- **boundary:** This subsection owns the assistant-service architecture and deterministic guardrails; S8.3 owns the conceptual geospatial schema and wrong-join example, while S8.4 owns voice confirmation and S9.1 owns source/data transformations.
- **source:** `research/nasa-data/T1.md`, lines 9-27 and 171-196; `research/site-prior-art/T6.md`, lines 51-63; external production-schema study https://doi.org/10.48550/arxiv.2610.03524 is a reference to verify, not proof of this proposed architecture.

### S9.4 Access reality: Earthdata Login, quotas, and what a judge can actually run
- **question:** Which parts of a demonstration can run without credentials, what requires Earthdata Login, how do latency and limits affect the demo, and what should the team relax first when a request fails?
- **reader:** A judge or teammate with a clean Linux/Python environment who needs reproducible commands and a no-login path that does not depend on a hidden account.
- **entry:** T1/T6 report free POWER access without login, open catalogue search, Earthdata Login for downloads of five recommended products, product-specific delays, and no granules downloaded during the research checks.
- **point:** Design two explicit paths: an actually public POWER/catalogue preflight and a credential-gated, small-subset Earthdata download path; expose retrieval time and latency rather than promising real-time data, and never fabricate live success or bypass authorization when the restricted path fails. Because the build envelope is large, keep the public path as the demo floor and let the credential path carry the full product set behind it, phased rather than trimmed.
- **chain:** Name the proposed build assumptions before commands: CPython 3.12, `earthaccess==0.19.0`, `requests`, and `rasterio==1.4.4` with bundled GDAL 3.10.3; the venv install and imports were exercised, but system `gdalinfo` and `gdalwarp` were absent, so use Rasterio's Python warp API unless a separate GDAL 3.x CLI build is installed; include the tested install/preflight sequence `python3.12 -m venv .venv && . .venv/bin/activate && python -m pip install earthaccess==0.19.0 requests rasterio==1.4.4 && python -c 'import earthaccess, rasterio; print(earthaccess.__version__, rasterio.__version__, rasterio.__gdal_version__)'`; the no-login POWER requests for a US point at 37.0,-98.0 and a Brazil point at -15.8,-56.1 returned HTTP 200 and 31 daily T2M values each for January 2025; the public SMAP CMR granule query returned HTTP 200 and five results; for download, require the user's own Earthdata Login configured through an existing `.netrc` or `EARTHDATA_USERNAME` and `EARTHDATA_PASSWORD` environment variables, call `earthaccess.login(strategy="environment")`, and never place credentials in source code, command history, or screenshots; report source latency from T1, including POWER's 2-3 day meteorological and 5-7 day solar typical latency, HLS's stated 1-2 day processing with observed roughly 3-day lag on the pack check date, SMAP L3's observed mean around 1 day 5 hours, ECOSTRESS JET's 2026-11-10 catalog lag of at least 13 days, and the GPM V07 Final stop after 2025-09-30; explain that CMR numeric quotas are not established in the packs and the report must not invent them.
- **principle:** A reproducible demo must have a credential-free path, an honest access boundary, a pinned environment, explicit data dates, and a documented fallback; no-login catalogue visibility does not mean download authorization.
- **instance:** The Python 3.12 environment installed `earthaccess==0.19.0`, `requests`, and `rasterio==1.4.4` with GDAL 3.10.3 bundled; system `gdalinfo` and `gdalwarp` were unavailable. Tested credential-free commands were `curl -fsSG 'https://power.larc.nasa.gov/api/temporal/daily/point' --data-urlencode 'parameters=T2M' --data-urlencode 'community=AG' --data-urlencode 'longitude=-98.0' --data-urlencode 'latitude=37.0' --data-urlencode 'start=20250101' --data-urlencode 'end=20250131' --data-urlencode 'format=JSON' --data-urlencode 'time-standard=UTC' -o power-us.json` and the same request with `--data-urlencode 'longitude=-56.1' --data-urlencode 'latitude=-15.8' -o power-br.json`; both returned HTTP 200 and 31 daily T2M values. The tested public catalogue command was `curl -fsSG 'https://cmr.earthdata.nasa.gov/search/granules.json' --data-urlencode 'collection_concept_id=C2938664763-NSIDC_CPRD' --data-urlencode 'page_size=5' --data-urlencode 'sort_key=-start_date' -o smap-cmr.json`; it returned HTTP 200 with five granules. These are endpoint checks only, not data downloads or farm-validation results. The Earthdata Login preflight `python -c 'import earthaccess; auth=earthaccess.login(strategy="environment"); assert auth.authenticated'` is runnable only after the user's credentials are configured in `.netrc` or `EARTHDATA_USERNAME` and `EARTHDATA_PASSWORD`; it was not run here.
- **limit:** T1 and T6 establish access routes and latency examples, not a universal current quota; the proposed package set and commands have not been run here, no Earthdata credentials or science granules were available, and T1 says the GPM V08 Final fall-2026 date was only projected.
