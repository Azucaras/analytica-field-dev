### S3.1 US soil survey: gSSURGO and gNATSGO at map-unit scale
- **question:** What do gSSURGO and gNATSGO provide for US soil context, and how should an app preserve the difference between map-unit evidence and a measured field soil profile?
- **reader:** App team joining soil layers to satellite pixels; may assume a 10 m or 30 m raster cell is an independent soil measurement.
- **entry:** A detailed US soil layer rendered as a grid, apparently aligned with 30 m imagery and ready to score field suitability.
- **point:** gSSURGO offers survey-derived map-unit attributes at 10 m state or 30 m CONUS raster display, while gNATSGO provides national continuity by mixing detailed surveys with generalized STATSGO2 and Raster Soil Surveys; raster-cell size does not make map units homogeneous or source quality uniform.
- **chain:** NRCS surveys map soil units and link unit keys to component, horizon and interpretation tables; many raster cells repeat the same map-unit key; component proportions and inclusions matter, so attribute aggregation needs an explicit weighted rule; gNATSGO fills detailed-survey gaps with sources of lower/generalized scale; Web Soil Survey can support human inspection for a particular area but is not an onsite diagnosis or stable bulk ingest API; preserve map-unit key, survey provenance, source class and interpretation depth when joining to imagery.
- **principle:** Survey scale, component composition and provenance bound the soil inference; a finer raster rendering does not imply independent field-level soil observations.
- **instance:** Use gSSURGO components and interpretations to construct an explainable US soil prior where detailed survey data exist; use gNATSGO for national continuity only while visibly suppressing precision where source coverage is generalized, and route important field decisions to Web Soil Survey review or sampling.
- **limit:** Neither product guarantees an onsite soil diagnosis or a uniform 10/30 m field soil measurement. gNATSGO's national raster mixes detailed and less detailed sources; no inference from grid-cell count to statistical sample count. Preserve the map-unit aggregation method and survey source, and do not apply US survey quality claims to Brazil.
- **budget:** 500 words.
- **boundary:** This subsection owns US soil survey databases and map-unit semantics; S3.2 owns Brazilian soil, land cover and property-registration context, and S3.4 owns cross-source licensing/privacy and 30 m joining implications.

### S3.2 Brazilian soil and land cover: PronaSolos, IBGE, SICAR, MapBiomas
- **question:** Which Brazilian products provide soil, land-cover and property-registration context, and what coverage or validation claims would overstate their evidence?
- **reader:** Team transferring a US soil-layer workflow to Brazil; may see a national portal or 90 m grid and infer US survey-equivalent field coverage.
- **entry:** A Brazilian field polygon overlaid with a national soil layer and annual 30 m land-cover history.
- **point:** Brazil has useful but heterogeneous survey, modeled-soil, sample and land-cover sources; none in this pack supports implying nationwide Brazilian soil coverage at US SSURGO survey quality, and finer raster posting does not supply missing observations.
- **chain:** IBGE Pedologia provides 1:250,000 sheet/map-unit coverage for regional priors; Embrapa GeoInfo is a 1:5,000,000 compilation; BDsolos/Redape contains uneven point profiles; PronaSolos is an ongoing programme and heterogeneous catalogue, with 90 m soil attributes modeled from legacy observations and digital mapping rather than observed at every cell; the reported federal summary says under 5% of Brazil had been mapped at 1:100,000 or finer; MapBiomas Collection 11 offers annual 30 m land cover through 2025 but collection reprocessing and class limitations affect historical interpretation; SICAR/CAR contains self-declared property registrations subject to state validation and is not a surveyed cadastral truth layer.
- **principle:** For Brazilian evidence, name the product, vintage, scale, modeling or sampling basis, footprint and licence separately; national availability is not uniform field-level coverage.
- **instance:** Use IBGE/Embrapa maps as regional soil priors, PronaSolos 90 m as modeled screening with provenance and uncertainty, Redape profiles for suitable local calibration, MapBiomas for broad land-cover/change context rather than certain crop identity, and SICAR only as an administrative self-declaration requiring verification.
- **limit:** Never describe PronaSolos target scales as already achieved coverage, MapBiomas as measured field soil, or SICAR boundaries as validated property lines. T2 finds no verified public nationwide 10 m Brazilian soil API or SSURGO-equivalent; specific state survey scale and dates vary, and several licences remain unclear or non-commercial. Do not assign Brazilian soil-map-unit or accuracy properties from US sources.
- **budget:** 700 words.
- **boundary:** This subsection owns Brazilian soil products, land-cover context and CAR boundary status; S3.1 owns US gSSURGO/gNATSGO, S3.3 owns crop-history and management sources, and S3.4 owns cross-cutting redistribution, privacy and grid joins.

### S3.3 Crop and management layers: CDL, CroplandCROS, ZARC, NASS
- **question:** Which crop and management layers can help establish crop sequence or planting-risk context in the USA and Brazil, and what can their records not reveal?
- **reader:** Product builder seeking annual crop history and actionable planting or management context from remotely sensed classifications and official statistics.
- **entry:** A crop-history table or map assembled from US CDL/CroplandCROS and Brazilian ZARC recommendations, with an apparent path to compare rotations.
- **point:** CDL and its viewer provide annual US crop-class evidence with changing resolution and classification limits; Brazilian ZARC supplies administrative municipality/crop/season/cultivar risk windows rather than field observations, and NASS statistics are US aggregates, so none alone proves a particular farm's management or rotation outcome.
- **chain:** CDL covers full CONUS from 2008, is 30 m through 2023 and 10 m from crop year 2024, and the 2025 layer was released 2026-02-27; annual classification can mislabel pixels, double crops may be combined, and pixel area is not official acreage; CroplandCROS is a viewer/export frontend to CDL, not an independent survey or field-boundary authority; crop sequence boundaries are algorithmically synthesized rather than cadastral; Crop Frequency Layers count presence without order or full sequence; ZARC portarias organize municipality, crop, cultivar group, planting decêndio and risk class with changing soil-class methodologies, and no machine API was found; Quick Stats and Census offer county/statistical aggregates, not farm-specific records.
- **principle:** Preserve crop year, class, source, resolution and method, and distinguish a classified crop observation or administrative recommendation from a record of actual farm management.
- **instance:** For a US prototype, retain CDL class and vintage to form candidate crop sequence, flag combined double-crop classes and ask the farmer to confirm history; in Brazil use the applicable season/crop/state ZARC portaria as a dated planting-risk screen, verifying cultivar and soil class rather than presenting it as parcel-level soil diagnosis; use NASS only for county benchmarks.
- **limit:** CDL classification is not complete management history or proof of yield, crop sequence boundaries are synthetic, Crop Frequency lacks order, Quick Stats/Census cannot reveal a specific farm, and ZARC is not a field soil test or substitute for current applicable portaria. The source pack does not provide a validated cross-country crop classifier, farm-level Brazilian crop history, or a unified schema; the countries' sources are not interchangeable.
- **budget:** 500 words.
- **boundary:** This subsection owns crop labels, sequence/frequency, planting-risk recommendations and agricultural statistics; S3.1–S3.2 own soil and land cover/boundary layers, while S3.4 owns licensing and the risks of joining them onto a 30 m grid.
- **source:** `/research/sources/T2.md` table lines 13–18 and 33–35; CDL lines 50–52; CroplandCROS lines 53–55; NASS Quick Stats lines 56–58; Brazilian ZARC lines 101–103; Crop Sequence Boundaries lines 119–121; Crop Frequency Layers lines 122–124; Census lines 125–127; US specifics lines 145–146; Brazil specifics lines 134, 138; What I could not find line 160.

### S3.4 Licence, privacy and the join problem down to 30 m
- **question:** What legal, privacy and semantic costs arise when joining non-NASA sources to 30 m satellite cells or redistributing a composite app layer?
- **reader:** Engineer or product lead designing a commercial or public-facing map and assuming public access plus spatial alignment means free reuse and equivalent detail.
- **entry:** A proposal to intersect field-like boundaries and soil/crop layers with 30 m imagery, publish a combined suitability map, and offer agent or map exports.
- **point:** Spatial joins can be computationally easy while being scientifically misleading, legally constrained and politically sensitive; a 30 m output grid cannot create 30 m source information or erase each input's licence, validation and privacy conditions.
- **chain:** Repeated gSSURGO cells encode map-unit keys, not separate soil observations; broad Brazilian polygons and 90 m modeled attributes acquire no finer information when resampled; map sources use different scales, vintages, classes and methods; each join must preserve source ID, vintage, scale, uncertainty and aggregation; Embrapa GeoInfo and BDsolos/Redape carry CC BY-NC terms, MapBiomas uses CC BY-SA, CONAB historical price reproduction is non-commercial, SGB 90 m lacks an exposed licence field, IBGE/INPE pages checked lack explicit dataset-level licences, and CAR is self-declared sensitive property geometry without verified open bulk redistribution rights; public access does not establish open commercial redistribution permission.
- **principle:** Treat every joined layer as a typed claim with its own geographic support, evidence class, vintage, licence, privacy risk and transformation history; preserve these properties through the map, SQL agent, voice answer and export.
- **instance:** A 30 m map can overlay original-resolution inputs for navigation, but should display provenance and suppress false precision, aggregate map-unit estimates transparently, maintain share-alike/non-commercial obligations, exclude or protect sensitive CAR geometry unless justified and permitted, and block commercial reuse pending confirmation of unclear licences.
- **limit:** T2 does not provide jurisdiction-specific legal advice or final permission determinations, and some licence pages did not state explicit terms. Do not conclude that public availability means public-domain reuse, or promise a complete national field-boundary layer; the CAR portal blocked this research host and no federal bulk API or blanket redistribution licence was verified.
- **budget:** 500 words.
- **boundary:** This subsection owns licences, privacy and cross-source spatial joining; S3.1–S3.3 own the content and source-specific interpretation of soil, crop and management products. Do not repeat each dataset description here.

## Open questions and risks
- No nationwide Brazilian 10 m field-scale soil equivalent to US SSURGO was found; less than 5% of Brazil was reported mapped at 1:100,000 or finer. Regional PronaSolos footprints, scales, vintages and underlying evidence need layer-by-layer verification before a field-level claim.
- T2 did not establish redistribution terms for IBGE, INPE, SGB/PronaSolos or all catalogue content. Obtain steward/legal confirmation before redistribution or commercial use; do not infer a licence from public access.
- SICAR/CAR automated access was denied and no federal bulk API or blanket redistribution permission was found. Coverage, state validation, overlap, accuracy and privacy controls need further checking; user-submitted boundaries are not cadastral truth.
- The ZARC machine API was not found. The exact current portaria and soil-class methodology must be checked by crop, state, cultivar and season; the pack does not offer a harmonized parser or field-level soil validation.
- CDL's 2024 resolution change, annual classification error, combined double crops and the difference between pixel counts and official acreage limit naive cross-vintage comparisons. No locally validated classifier is included.
- The source pack describes public and commercial use constraints but does not provide legal advice or a definitive legal review of derivative layers. An app's business model and export behavior determine which terms need review.
- A 30 m grid can be a display or join target only; report the native support and do not relabel repeated map units, 90 m modeled attributes, county statistics or broad polygons as 30 m observations.
