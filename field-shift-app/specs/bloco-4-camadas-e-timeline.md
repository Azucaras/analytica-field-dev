## Problem

Blocks 1 to 3 give the screen a fixed frame and one satellite layer. Every other layer the research names (three SMAP products for soil moisture, two rain cadences, vegetation, surface temperature, optical imagery, GRACE) cannot be added without a second code path, because the proxy hardcodes one tile matrix and one five-day lookback. The season selector gives the user a menu of crop years that the map does not use. The timeline replaces it with one day axis, so every layer shows the date it really has, its age, and whether it has an observation for the chosen day. Block 4 is the foundation for blocks 5 to 8. It defines the layer registry, the effective-date rule and the date axis that the polygon, number and agent blocks inherit (ROTEIRO-BLOCOS-4-A-8.md, section 3). The rotation outlook panel, which shows nothing relevant to the user today, can be collapsed so that the timeline and the map have the room.

## Users and scope

- Users: the producer who reads the map, and the judge who checks that no layer claims a date it does not have.
- In scope: a registry of 13 GIBS entries with a matrix per layer; per-layer lookback; the effective date and its age on every layer; a per-layer pin; opacity and order; a two-moment compare for tile layers; raster sanity and degraded mode for the new layers; the timeline dock with a date picker that replaces the season control; the availability track; the season band derived from the date and read from the ZARC snapshot; the optical coverage pick; the 360 toggle; the collapse toggle of the rotation outlook panel; and the basemap adapter interface, with no rendering change.
- Out of scope: F-06 animation over dates (removed by user decision, CO-B4-17); polygons and the satellite basemap rendering (block 5, F-26 to F-30); POWER series, normals, percentiles and numeric deltas (block 6, F-08, F-09, F-34 numeric half, F-35); the soil card and rotation history (block 6); the content of the rotation cards and the fixture scenario data (block 3 CO-07, CO-B4-13); producer input (block 7); the agent and voice (block 8); ECOSTRESS (no GIBS layer, and the Earthdata login sits outside the critical path). See Carry-over.

## Game UX rationale

Source: Game UX.md (thumb zone, clear centre, progressive disclosure, peripheral anchoring), as block 3 cites it.

| Control | Wild Rift principle | One line |
|---|---|---|
| Timeline dock | Peripheral anchoring | One strip sets the moment for the whole screen, and it never enters the clear centre. |
| Date picker (`Go to date`) | Detail on demand | A typed or picked day jumps straight to any date on the axis, so a far day costs no drag. |
| Previous day, Next day | Thumb zone | Two 44 px buttons step one observation day at a time, for a thumb that cannot drag. |
| Range row (`Timeline date`) | Thumb zone | The range moves one day per arrow and 30 days per page key, along the same row as the step buttons. |
| Timeline toggle at 360 and 780 (`Timeline controls`) | Progressive disclosure | At 360 the dock is one 44 px toggle until opened, and a 44 px toggle stays beside the open dock so it can be folded again; at 780 the dock is open and the same toggle folds it away when the panel or the map needs the room. |
| Season band | Context on the edge | The crop calendar sits on the timeline, so the season reads as a position in time and not as a menu. |
| Availability track | Honest peripheral signal | The track marks the days that have an observation, so an empty day is never read as a clear day. |
| Layer row, collapsed | Progressive disclosure | One line per layer gives its name, basis, date and age; opacity, order, pin and compare wait behind one press. |
| Opacity, order, pin, compare | Detail on demand | Fine controls cost nothing until the layer is opened, so the default view stays quiet. |
| Compare divider | Clear centre | Compare is off at first paint. When it is on, the divider is the only mark across the centre of the map. |
| Rotation outlook collapse (`Collapse Rotation outlook`) | Progressive disclosure | The panel shows its header and nothing else when collapsed, so the timeline and the map get the room; one press brings the cards back. |

## Requirements

Types follow blocks 2 and 3: ubiquitous, state-driven, event-driven, unwanted behaviour. R-21 is withdrawn by user decision (F-06 removed from block 4); its number is kept so that no other number moves.

### Registry and tile contract

- R-01 (ubiquitous): The backend SHALL define each raster layer once, in a registry that carries the fields listed in Data contracts. No other module SHALL hold a layer id, a GIBS identifier or a matrix. The block 1 id `smap-soil-moisture` SHALL remain an alias of `smap-l3`, so the block 1 requests and the reserve file still resolve.
- R-02 (ubiquitous): The upstream tile URL SHALL use the matrix declared by the layer's registry entry. The literal `GoogleMapsCompatible_Level6` in `_upstream_url` SHALL be removed, because a wrong matrix answers HTTP 400 for every tile (M-01).
- R-03 (ubiquitous): Every registry `gibs_id` SHALL appear in the identifier list saved as `artifacts/gibs-identifiers-2026-11-14.txt`, which has 1330 lines at its fetch (M-16). The registry SHALL NOT contain ECOSTRESS. LPRM AMSR2 and the NDH drought map are probe-verified and are carry-over items (CO-B4-01, CO-B4-02), not registry entries.
- R-04 (state-driven): While a layer's `kind` is `stale`, the layer SHALL show `stale` with its last observation date and SHALL NOT show any date as current at any chosen date. GRACE is the only stale layer, and its last observation is 2022-07-01.
- R-05 (event-driven): When a layer is requested for a chosen date c, its effective date SHALL be the greatest candidate d on or before c, within lookback_days of c, for which the upstream answers HTTP 200 and the body is an observation under the next clause. Candidates SHALL follow the cadence of the layer, SHALL start at the lesser of c and declared_end (for a `datetime` layer, the lesser of the end of the UTC day of c and declared_end), and SHALL NOT precede time_start. For a layer whose `optical` field is true, a 200 body of 5,000 bytes or less SHALL NOT count as an observation, and the walk SHALL continue (OD-11, M-17). The walk and its order are the Data contracts rule.
- R-06 (ubiquitous): Whenever the effective date differs from the chosen date, the layer SHALL show the no-observation marker, the effective date and its age in days. When the two dates are equal, the marker SHALL NOT appear. The reading of consequence 2 that this clause encodes is OD-18, which the user accepts at freeze.
- R-07 (unwanted behaviour): If no candidate answers HTTP 200 within the lookback, then the layer SHALL report `none_in_lookback`, SHALL serve no tile for that date, and SHALL NOT keep a previous date's tile on screen.
- R-08 (ubiquitous): The lookback SHALL be read per layer from the registry. The shared `for back in range(5)` loop at `backend/main.py` line 182 SHALL be removed, and the effective date walk of R-05 SHALL issue no request for a date outside [time_start, declared_end]. Two scans are exempt from the declared_end bound and are bounded by the chosen date instead, because a declared end lags the imagery: the `Latest image` scan of R-44 SHALL request the dates from the chosen date backwards even when the chosen date lies after declared_end, and `GET /layers/{id}/coverage` SHALL request every date of its window, which ends at the chosen date, even when those dates lie after declared_end. Neither scan SHALL request a date before time_start. The walk, the scan and the coverage route therefore agree: only the walk clamps to declared_end (AC-07, AC-35, AC-41).
- R-09 (ubiquitous): A layer with `time_format` `datetime` (IMERG 30 min) SHALL be requested with a token of the form `YYYY-MM-DDTHH:MM:SSZ` on a 30 minute grid. A chosen calendar date for such a layer SHALL resolve, through its declared_end, to the registry value of Table B. A date-only token SHALL NOT be sent to it. Every date in this block is a UTC date.
- R-10 (unwanted behaviour): If the upstream answers a status other than 200, 400 or 404, or cannot be reached, then the backend SHALL NOT walk the lookback and SHALL report `unavailable`. This keeps the behaviour of `backend/main.py` lines 193 to 196 and 252 to 256.
- R-11 (ubiquitous): Upstream HTTP 400 and HTTP 404 SHALL each step the walk to the next candidate. An unknown layer id SHALL be refused by the backend's own router with HTTP 404 before any upstream request, as `get_tile` does today (line 236). An upstream 404 SHALL NOT be returned to the client as the layer's status.
- R-12 (ubiquitous): Every HTTP 200 tile response SHALL carry `X-Tile-Date` with the effective date, including responses served from the cache. The cache key SHALL be layer, effective date, z, x and y. The cache hit at `backend/main.py` lines 245 to 249 drops the date today, and that is corrected here.
- R-13 (event-driven): When `GET /tiles/{layer}/{z}/{x}/{y}?date=YYYY-MM-DD` is requested, the backend SHALL resolve the effective date by R-05 from the given date and SHALL return it in `X-Tile-Date`. A date after declared_end SHALL resolve to the last valid candidate, so `smap-l3` at 2026-11-12 resolves to 2026-11-11.
- R-14 (ubiquitous): `GET /layers?date=YYYY-MM-DD&z=&x=&y=` SHALL return the object defined in Data contracts, not a JSON array. `GET /layers/{id}/coverage?days=N` SHALL return the optical coverage of R-44. The coverage route SHALL answer HTTP 400 for every layer whose `optical` field is false, which includes `smap-l3` and `grace-mascon`.
- R-15 (unwanted behaviour): If z is greater than the layer's max_zoom, then the backend SHALL answer HTTP 400 with the text `Zoom above max_zoom for layer` and SHALL NOT issue an upstream request. The parent-tile path at `backend/main.py` lines 178 to 180 and 188 returns the whole parent tile uncropped, so it SHALL be removed. It is latent today, because the front never asks above zoom 6.
- R-16 (ubiquitous): The front raster source of each layer SHALL set `maxzoom` to the registry's max_zoom, replacing the literal 6 at `src/components/MapCanvas.tsx` line 40.

### Layers, order, compare and degraded mode

- R-17 (ubiquitous): Each layer SHALL have an on or off state and an opacity between 0 and 1. A layer turned on SHALL start at opacity 0.55, the value `MapCanvas.tsx` line 79 applies today. Opacity SHALL change with `setPaintProperty` on `raster-opacity` and SHALL NOT remove or re-add the source.
- R-18 (ubiquitous): Layer order SHALL be a stack list, with the first item drawn on top. A move SHALL reorder the style layers with `moveLayer` and SHALL NOT remove any source. The order SHALL survive a change of composition preset.
- R-19 (ubiquitous): A layer that is off SHALL issue no tile request. Turning a layer on SHALL issue requests only for the viewport tiles at the chosen date.
- R-20 (event-driven): When the chosen date changes, each layer that is on and not pinned SHALL re-resolve its effective date and SHALL request tiles only if the effective date changed. A layer pinned by its own date (R-26) SHALL keep its pin until the pin is removed, and the row SHALL show the word `pinned`.
- R-22 (event-driven): When compare is turned on for one tile layer (R-26 control), the map SHALL show two dates of that layer side by side, divided by a control named `Compare divider` that moves with the keyboard (AC-38) and with the pointer (AC-18). Side A SHALL be the chosen date. Side B SHALL be set by the `Compare date, {layer name}` date input, which defaults to seven days before the chosen date, clamped to the axis start (a design default, not a measured value). Each side SHALL carry its own date and age label. Moving the divider SHALL NOT change the centre or the zoom. The numeric delta of F-34 is carried to block 6 (CO-B4-04).
- R-23 (unwanted behaviour): If a tile answers with an `X-Tile-Date` later than the chosen date, then the app SHALL reject it and SHALL show `none_in_lookback` for that layer. If a 200 response has zero bytes, then the app SHALL NOT draw it and SHALL show `unavailable`. This is the raster half of F-24. The POWER fill value belongs to block 6.
- R-24 (unwanted behaviour): If the `/layers` request fails, or a layer's status is `unavailable`, then the rail SHALL keep that layer's row, SHALL print the status text, and SHALL NOT print a date or an age for it. The row SHALL NOT disappear. This replaces the silent keep-previous behaviour at `src/components/LayerRail.tsx` lines 32 to 35.
- R-25 (unwanted behaviour): If the upstream is unavailable (a 5xx status or a connection error), then the proxy SHALL serve, in this order: the most recent cached tile of the same layer and tile address dated on or before the chosen date, whatever its age, even beyond the 24-hour TTL of block 1 R-22; then the reserve tile of the same layer, if one exists. A cached entry dated after the chosen date SHALL NOT be served, which keeps R-23. The app SHALL label the result with its date, or with `offline sample, date unknown` when no date is recorded. A layer with neither a cached tile nor a reserve SHALL show `no offline copy`.
- R-26 (ubiquitous): Each rail row SHALL show the layer name, its basis, its resolution, its effective date, its age, the marker of R-06 and its status. The row SHALL be collapsed by default and SHALL expand through a button of at least 44 by 44 px named `Layer options, {layer name}`. The expanded row SHALL carry, in this order: an opacity range named `Opacity, {layer name}` (R-17; arrow keys step 0.05, a design target); two move buttons named `Move {layer name} up` and `Move {layer name} down` (R-18); a pin toggle named `Pin date, {layer name}` with `aria-pressed` (R-20); the availability track of that layer (R-33); and, for a tile layer, a compare toggle named `Compare, {layer name}` with `aria-pressed` (R-22). Every control in the expanded row SHALL measure at least 44 by 44 px.

### Timeline, season and availability

- R-27 (ubiquitous): The timeline dock SHALL replace the `SeasonSelect` component. No element named `Season` and no `select` for the season SHALL remain in the DOM.
- R-28 (ubiquitous): The axis SHALL run from 2015-04-01 to the axis end, which is the latest declared_end of any registry layer (2026-11-14 at this revision, from lst-aqua and from imerg-30min at its Table B value, both on that day). The chosen date SHALL default to the axis end. The query `?date=YYYY-MM-DD` SHALL set the chosen date at load when the date lies on the axis, so browser measurements can pin it. A date outside the axis SHALL clamp to the nearest bound and SHALL show `Date limited to {date}` in a polite live region.
- R-29 (ubiquitous): The crop season SHALL be derived from the chosen date. A season starts on 1 July, so the label is `Y/YY`, where Y is the year of the most recent 1 July on or before the date (OD-02).
- R-30 (state-driven): While the derived season differs from the fixture's `default_season` of 2025/26, the scenario and evidence values shown by block 3 SHALL keep showing the fixture scenario and SHALL print the label `Fixture data for 2025/26, not for {derived season}`. The scenario values SHALL NOT change with the date, because the fixture evidence rows carry no season field.
- R-31 (ubiquitous): The season band SHALL draw the planting windows that the ZARC snapshot `artifacts/zarc-mt-snapshot.json` holds for the field's municipality, for each crop of the field, and for the derived season of the chosen date only (R-29). The band's season key SHALL be the derived label of R-29 in the form `Y/YY`. The snapshot writes its season in the form `Y/YYYY` (`2025/2026`, `2026/2027`), so the band SHALL normalise the derived label to the snapshot key `{Y}/{Y+1}` written with four digits before it reads a row, and the label drawn on the band and the label inside the empty text SHALL both be the derived `Y/YY` form (`2026/27` for the snapshot key `2026/2027`). The band SHALL draw the windows of that one season and SHALL NOT draw the windows of the other season the snapshot holds. The municipality and UF are read from the field's `location` text (`Santa Carmem, Mato Grosso` in the fixture gives municipality `Santa Carmem` and UF `MT`) and matched to the snapshot's `municipality` and `uf` fields. The crops are the crops of the field's scenarios (`Soybean`, `Corn` and `Cotton` in the fixture), mapped by the table "Crop and season mapping for the band" in Data contracts. The window SHALL be the JSON key `window_risk_20` (cells equal to 20). Because the snapshot holds 93 rows over soil codes and cycle codes, the band SHALL draw, for each crop and season, the envelope of those rows: the earliest `start_date` and the latest `end_date` of `window_risk_20` over the rows with that crop and season (OD-22). The field record carries no soil code and no cycle (`backend/fixtures/field-scenarios.json` holds only `location` and the crops, M-22), so no single row can be chosen. The band SHALL show `No planting window data for this municipality` only when the snapshot does not hold the municipality, `No planting window data for {crop}` only for a crop it does not hold for that municipality, and `No planting window data for {season}` for a season it does not hold, where `{season}` is the derived `Y/YY` label and not the snapshot's four digit form, so 2024-11-01 gives `No planting window data for 2024/25` (OD-21). The band claims no window content that the snapshot does not hold.
- R-32 (ubiquitous): Each season record SHALL carry a `crop_class` slot, and the class SHALL change at the season boundary of R-29. Block 4 has no class source, because the MapBiomas access is blocked (PESQUISA section 11.2). The slot SHALL show `No crop class source` in this block (OD-04, accepted by the user).
- R-33 (ubiquitous): The dock SHALL carry one availability track, the primary track, over the visible axis range. The primary layer is the first layer that is on, in stack order (R-18), or `smap-l3` when no layer is on. The primary track, the age readout of R-37, the `aria-valuetext` of R-37 and the axis readout use the primary layer. Each other visible layer SHALL carry its own availability track in its expanded rail row (R-26). The Sentinel-2 track, which is track-only, SHALL sit in the expanded row of `hls-l30` and `hls-s30`. Marks SHALL come only from the recorded declared ranges of that layer, and a day inside a gap SHALL carry no mark. The Sentinel-2 track SHALL draw its marks from a recorded STAC response and SHALL highlight the scenes with cloud cover below 20 percent.
- R-34 (ubiquitous): For a date before a layer's time_start, the layer SHALL show `none_in_lookback`, and its track SHALL carry no mark before time_start. NDVI and EVI are declared from 2025-02-12 in the capabilities, so they show no observation for 2015-04-01 to 2025-02-11 (M-05).

### Effective-date rule and worked example

The effective date is the last observation on or before the chosen date, found by the walk below. For a chosen date c and a tile layer L:

1. Set the start to the lesser of c and declared_end. For a `datetime` layer, use the end of the UTC day of c, then take the lesser with declared_end.
2. Build the candidate list by stepping back from the start by the cadence: one day for P1D; to the first day of each earlier month for P1M; 30 minutes for PT30M. Stop when the candidate is earlier than the later of time_start and c minus lookback_days.
3. Request the candidate. HTTP 200 with a body above `blank_threshold_bytes` when the layer is optical, or any HTTP 200 otherwise: the effective date is the candidate, and the walk ends. HTTP 200 with a body at or below the threshold of an optical layer, HTTP 400 or HTTP 404: go to the next candidate. Any other status, or a connection error: stop and report `unavailable` (R-10).
4. If the list is exhausted without a 200 that counts: `none_in_lookback`.
5. Status: `on_date` when the effective date equals c; `earlier` when it is before c; `stale` for a stale kind; `unavailable` or `none_in_lookback` as above.

Worked example for chosen date 2026-11-12, three layers, measured on 2026-11-14 with the curl commands below:

| smap-l3 | 2026-11-11 (2026-11-12 lies after declared_end 2026-11-11 and is not requested) | 200, 9999 bytes | 2026-11-11 | 1 | earlier |

### Season derivation

`seasonForDate(d)` returns `Y/YY`, where `Y = d.year` if `d` is on or after 1 July of `d.year`, and `Y = d.year - 1` otherwise, and the second part is `(Y + 1) mod 100` with two digits. Examples used by AC-24: 2015-04-01 gives `2014/15`; 2025-06-30 gives `2024/25`; 2025-07-01 gives `2025/26`; 2026-11-12 gives `2026/27`. The ZARC snapshot's season key for a label `Y/YY` is `{Y}/{Y+1}` written with four digits in the second part, so `2026/27` reads `2026/2027` and `2025/26` reads `2025/2026`. That normalisation is the only conversion between the two forms (R-31, AC-25 d), and the derived label is what the band and the empty text print.
