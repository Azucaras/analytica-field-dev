## Problem

Block 4 draws planting windows on a band for the selected polygon, and block 6 prints numbers for that polygon. A producer still cannot record the field he actually farms, declare what he did on it, or read the window as a calendar that says, for each limit, who set it and when. The polygon is somebody else's boundary, and the window is a band on a timeline rather than a table a person can read against his own sowing date.

Block 7 adds the two things the repository can fill today: the field record, where a producer draws or imports his own boundary and declares his own seasons, and the planting calendar, which reads block 4's window source and block 6's numbers and prints every limit with its portaria. The trap it is written against is the one that already bit this project once: a form whose controls had no consumer, whose values persisted nowhere and whose unknown default hid a measured value. Every stored value in this block therefore names its consumer, its unit, its storage path and the criterion that fails when the consumer is gone, and the three economic fields fail that bar and are dropped rather than shipped (M-19).

The second trap is the advised decision, and the honest answer to it is that it does not exist yet. Of the four criteria the comparator would weigh, one is a measured field level number and two have no derivation in this repository at all, so it would rank a single measurement against producer declarations; the three fixture fields also share one climate cell, so the one measured criterion cannot separate their scenarios (M-06, M-07, M-08). A surface that opens on an abstention is not a decision aid, and stacking the calendar behind it was the wrong order. That is why the comparator and the simulator are carried to block 8 with their requirements intact rather than softened here.

Consumer bar, stated once for both records: every key that `fs.fields.v1` or `fs.producer.v1` holds, whether the producer typed it or the store wrote it, names at least one consumer below, and the criterion named there fails when that consumer is missing. `schema_version` is read on every load, and its consumer is the version gate of R-54 (AC-47). `id` is the argument of the `select_field` command (R-41, AC-37), the key of the producer entry that holds a field's history (R-42, AC-38) and the field key of the block 6 request. `name` is the chip label, the delete confirmation and the name taken rule (Table A, AC-38). `ring` is the query area of blocks 5 and 6, and the ring every test of R-38 reads (AC-34). `area_ha` is the `area_ha` of the body of `POST /fields/{field_id}/numbers` (block 6 R-45, AC-37) and the printed area of R-57(d) (AC-36). `region` is the region test of R-41 and the text of V-21 (AC-37). `source` and `source_file` are the import provenance printed on the field record card (Table A, AC-33). `created_at` and `updated_at` have no screen consumer; their consumer is the export of R-56, which carries them for every field (AC-49).

Area formula, the one R-38 and R-40 use and the one the example reproduces. Angles are in radians, R is 6,378,137 m (the WGS 84 equatorial radius), and the ring has n distinct vertices, its closing repeated point removed, numbered 0 to n-1 with indices taken modulo n. Then S is the sum over i from 0 to n-1 of (lon[i+2] - lon[i]) * sin(lat[i+1]), and area_m2 = |S| * R * R / 2. The two unit conversions are: degrees to radians multiply by pi/180, and square metres to hectares divide by 10,000. Block 6's `ring_area` row names the spherical area without a formula, so this block pins this one, and block 6's implementation must use the same form.

For the example ring of Table C, the four distinct vertices are at longitudes -55.285 and -55.26 and latitudes -11.9 and -11.92. S = 2.980599e-7 and area_m2 = 6,062,632.76, so area_ha = 606.2633, stored at full precision and printed as 606.3 ha. The check is planar: at mean latitude -11.91 the box is 2,723.08 m by 2,226.39 m, which is also 606.2633 ha. The value 22.4 that revision 3 stored is the size of a median polygon of block 6, not the area of this ring, and it was 27.07 times too small.

The region extents used by R-40 are the two the frozen blocks already hold: Brazil is the Trazo3 extract box `-61.633, -18.0416, -50.2248, -7.3487` of block 5 R-10, and the United States is the CSB pilot box `-97.20, 46.90, -97.14, 46.96` of the same requirement.
